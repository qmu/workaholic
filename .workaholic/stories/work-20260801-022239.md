## 1. Overview

Missions had become hard to *finish*: six active ones sat at 0 of 3–9 acceptance criteria. The gates were not too strict — nothing bounded how much a mission may say, so an acceptance list grows into an audit list nobody can honestly tick. This branch adds the missing ceiling.

**Highlights:**

1. `## Scope` is deleted from the mission template and from `/propose`'s draft scaffold — no validator, script, or hook ever read it.
2. `## Acceptance` is normatively three items or fewer, measured by a new `mission/scripts/size.sh` alongside a 60-line / 2 KB document ceiling.
3. The ceiling is a **norm** for a human author and a **hard limit** for the unattended batch — and the approved floor is untouched, which a test now proves.

## 2. Motivation

A mission that cannot be ticked cannot be closed, and the four missions open at the start of this branch had accumulated acceptance lists sized like audit lists. The instruction behind the ticket was explicit that the problem is the absence of a ceiling, not the strictness of the floor.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. Cap mission size, drop the Scope section, and hold /propose's drafts to the same ceiling ([a9215d13](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/a9215d13))

`create.sh` and `scaffold-draft.sh` no longer emit `## Scope`; `close.sh`'s now-dead carry rule for it was removed. `size.sh` reports `{lines, bytes, acceptance_items, within}`, scoping its checklist count to `## Acceptance` exactly as every other mission reader does. The feedback skill gains the matching one-paragraph norm. `mission/SKILL.md` and `CLAUDE.md` carry the rules and the reasoning.

## 4. Outcome

A mission written from today's template is smaller by construction, and the three-item acceptance rule gives an author a concrete target for "the minimum conditions under which this is done". Legacy missions keep their `## Scope` as history and are never retro-blocked.

## 5. Historical Analysis

The same shape as the `strategies/` retirement and the `## Reflection` removal: a section that existed for a plausible reason, was read by nothing, and cost every author who filled it. The consistent treatment is deletion from the template plus verbatim preservation of history.

## 6. Concerns

### The size norms are unmeasured for human-authored missions until something calls size.sh

- **Severity:** moderate
- **Description:** `size.sh` reports rather than blocks for a human author, by design — but nothing yet calls it during the `/mission` interrogation, so the norm currently relies on the author reading `mission/SKILL.md` (`plugins/workaholic/skills/mission/scripts/size.sh`).
- **How to Fix:** Have the Creation Interrogation and the Replan flow run `size.sh` before publishing and show the measurement. That is a `/mission` command change, deliberately not bundled here.

### Six existing missions are over the new ceiling

- **Severity:** low
- **Description:** Measured now, `adopt-a-git-flow-branching-model-with-durable-ship-records` is 151 lines / 9 KB / 8 acceptance items against 60 / 2048 / 3. Nothing rewrites them, which is correct — history is not migrated — but the roadmap still shows lists that will not get ticked.
- **How to Fix:** Replan is the sanctioned path: `/mission "<instruction>"` can drop now-moot criteria, and each drop is recorded as its own changelog line. That is the developer's call per mission, not a sweep.

## 7. Successful Development Patterns

- Deciding norm-vs-gate by asking *who holds the pen* rather than *how important is the rule*. A human author is present and exercising judgment, so a refusal would fire on legitimately larger work; an unattended batch has no judgment to exercise, so guidance there is an unenforced wish. The same question resolves the next rule that must cover both writers.
- Letting the existing suite adjudicate a claim in new prose. The skill asserted that `close.sh` would still carry a legacy `## Scope`; a failing test showed the rule could never fire, because the successor is scaffolded from the template that no longer has the heading. The dead rule was removed and the prose corrected rather than the test deleted.
