## 1. Overview

The whole mission that gives a mission a lower bound: **created with two or more tickets, or it is not a mission**. This branch decides the boundary, enforces it at all four creation seams, resolves the two instances already on record, and makes every document that describes a mission state the floor. The mission's `## Acceptance` is 3/3.

## 2. Motivation

"Feedback", "ticket" and "mission" are only distinguishable if the middle one has a floor. Without it a ticketless mission is a feedback record on the roadmap and a one-ticket mission is a ticket with a progress bar — and two of eleven missions ever created were in exactly those states, both minted by seams that create without emitting tickets.

The branch ran over three passes because one seam turned out to be sequenced behind a data-loss bug. That sequencing is the story's spine and is recorded at each step below rather than smoothed over: the refusal that completes the floor could not ship until the carry route it would have left as the only survivor stopped dropping its payload.

**This story spans two pull requests.** Sections 3-1 to 3-3 merged as [#189](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/pull/189) at 11:01 UTC, while the third pass was still driving; 3-4 and 3-5 were pushed to the same branch afterwards and merge separately. The story is kept whole because it is the branch's record, not a pull request's.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. Decide the mission ticket floor and the carry ([b0717bd7](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/b0717bd7))

Three questions answered in `mission/SKILL.md` under **Granularity → The ticket floor**, where the "what makes a mission a mission" rule already lives, so the floor reads as the granularity table's bottom edge rather than a separate lint:

- **What counts, and when** — tickets naming the mission, present in the *same publication commit*, counted at the **publish seam**. Not at the write of `mission.md`: a `PostToolUse` hook fires before the interrogation has emitted anything, so a write-time floor refuses the normal authoring order. The 37-unlinked-acceptance-items measurement is cited as the already-paid cost of checking where the data does not yet exist.
- **Exactly two, refused not warned** — with the archived one-ticket instance named so the rule is not misread as a judgment on it.
- **The carry** — `--successor-title` refused; a carry names an existing mission. Rejected options and their costs are in `reference/schema.md`.

Two edits beyond the four steps, required by the ticket's own read-back criterion: the carry doctrine and the reorganize-and-carry recipe both described `--successor-title` as live, which would have left the document contradicting itself exactly where a reader checks.

### 3-2. Add the mission ticket floor verdict ([5b067dbe](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/5b067dbe))

**One counter, not four.** `queue-size.sh` already computed `total`, which *is* the quantity the floor is about, so the verdict rides there as `floor` / `meets_floor` rather than becoming a third count. Four seams read it; four inline counts would drift, and the drift would be invisible because each seam is exercised separately. It counts `total`, not `todo` — a fully-driven mission still satisfies the floor, because the floor is about how a mission was *created*.

**Computed, not enforced, in the reader.** `queue-size.sh` is a pure read called from drivability checks too; exiting non-zero on a sub-floor mission would break all of them.

**Three seams wired:** the Creation Interrogation states the floor *during* the rounds; `/propose` drops a candidate it cannot decompose and reports the drop rather than staying silently quiet; `create.sh` and `scaffold-draft.sh` carry documented exemptions explaining that a scaffold writer runs before any ticket exists — required so their absence does not read as an oversight.

### 3-3. Carry the remainder into an existing mission ([d8da7446](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/d8da7446))

The blocker, found while implementing the fourth seam and then reverted rather than shipped. `close.sh`'s unmet-acceptance inheritance lived **entirely inside its mint branch**; `--successor <slug>` resolved a path, set a variable and fell through — inheriting nothing — while `CLAUDE.md` and `mission/SKILL.md` both stated it "already carries the unmet items". A carry archived its predecessor, named a successor, and dropped every unmet item while reporting success.

Refusing `--successor-title` first would therefore have left **no** carry route that transfers the remainder: a record defect traded for a data-loss one. So the extraction moved to `mission/scripts/lib/acceptance.sh` (`mission_unchecked_items`, one reader, because two copies of "which items are unmet" is the drift that produced the silent drop), and the existing-successor route gained a real append — markers intact, idempotent by item text, `carried_from` becoming a list under a second carry, and the successor's own Goal, gates and items untouched.

### 3-4. Refuse a minted carry successor ([d04124d8](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/d04124d8))

The fourth seam, now unblocked. `--successor-title` is refused at argument validation with `successor_title_refused`, and the refusal **names the route** — create the successor through `/mission "<title>"`, whose interrogation emits its ticket set, then carry into it with `--successor <slug>`. `carried_needs_successor` gained the same treatment. A refusal that names only the rule leaves the author to retry the same thing.

**The mint branch was deleted, not left unreachable.** With the flag refused at validation, its `slug.sh` call, its `create.sh` call and its ~75-line awk rebuild could never execute again. What it knew — that it *rebuilt* a scaffold, importing Goal and `gate_*` because it owned the whole file, against the surviving route's *append* — is recorded in `close.sh`'s header and `reference/schema.md`, which is where a reader looks. The test that specified the mint route became a refusal test asserting the refusal is total: nothing minted, nothing archived, no status flip, the acceptance list untouched.

### 3-5. State the ticket floor in every doc ([f7e62837](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/f7e62837))

Enforcement stops new violations and does nothing about the two on record.

**The live one was fixed; the archived one was annotated.** `make-the-branch-story-measurably-shorter` (active, 0 tickets, minted by the seam 3-4 refuses) was replanned into the two-ticket set its own acceptance items already implied — measure which story sections carry the 29% growth, then cut what the measurement named and prove the number moved — with both acceptance links stamped through `link-acceptance.sh`. `drop-the-draft-gate-and-make-drive-own-its-worktree-from-refreshed-main` (archived, 1 ticket) got a feedback record naming it the pre-rule instance; the archived file is byte-identical.

**The replan was then dropped in favour of `main`'s.** A parallel lane landed the identical replan as [#202](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/pull/202) (`20260804201653-*`) while this branch was driving, so the catch-up merge conflicted on the same two acceptance items with different ticket ids. `main`'s pair is authoritative and this branch's two tickets were deleted — the mission still reports `meets_floor: true` with `0` unlinked, from `main`'s set. The floor was reached twice and counted once; what remains from this ticket is the annotation, the five documents, and the fix to the live tree.

**Five documents now state the floor**: `CLAUDE.md` (the Sources paragraph and the `/mission` row), `mission/SKILL.md` (the Redefinition record's current meaning, plus both instances in the floor section), `propose/SKILL.md` (already carried it from 3-2), `.workaholic/README.md`, and `rules/workaholic.md`'s layout table.

## 5. Concerns

### `.workaholic/README.md` was two rules out of date, and nothing had noticed

Its `missions/` entry still described `status: draft`, `status: approved` and `/mission approve <slug>` — all retired on 2026-07-31 — and told a reader that a mission publishes "to `main` in one commit", superseded by J4's publish-behind-a-PR the same week. Fixed here because it sat in the same sentence the floor had to be written into, but the staleness was independent of this mission. The file is a top-level orientation document for anyone entering the tree; that it drifted two decisions behind without surfacing suggests it is not on any doc-drift path, and `/report`'s `doc-drift.sh` backstop did not raise it.

### Two lanes replanned the same mission within twenty minutes

This branch and [#202](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/pull/202) both emitted a ticket set for `make-the-branch-story-measurably-shorter`, independently, reaching the same two-ticket decomposition with different filenames. Nothing prevented it: the mission had no claim (it was `no_tickets`-excluded from every survey, so no runner could claim it), and a replan is not a claimable unit, so two lanes fixing the same sub-floor mission were invisible to each other. The duplicate cost was small and caught by the merge, but the mechanism is general — any two sessions acting on an unclaimable mission will collide silently, and the second one to merge pays.

### A pull request merged mid-run, and the run kept pushing to its branch

`#189` merged at 11:01 UTC at the branch's then-tip while the third pass was mid-ticket. The four commits pushed afterwards were stranded on a branch whose pull request was closed — recoverable only because the branch was still there. The claim reader also stopped seeing the unit: `claims_scan` keys on the newest `Claim` commit **in the unmerged range**, and that commit had reached `main` with the merge, so a branch with four unmerged commits and live work read as claimless and the mission was offered as fresh. Nothing double-picked it in practice, but the window was real.

### `mission/SKILL.md` is at 417 of its 420-line cap

Unchanged from the first pass — every edit in 3-4 and 3-5 was written to fit rather than to grow, and detail went to `reference/schema.md` as the convention directs. It leaves three lines of headroom for the next author, who should expect to relocate rather than discover the gate.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

**When a change forces you to delete a specification, suspect the change.** Shipping the refusal in the first pass would have required deleting ~50 lines of assertions describing inheritance behavior. That was the signal that the sequencing was wrong, not that the tests were stale — and once the behavior had a home in `lib/acceptance.sh` with its own test against the surviving route, the same deletion cost nothing.

**A sub-floor artifact that is live is fixed; one that is archived is annotated.** The area decides the treatment, not the severity. Editing an archived mission to satisfy a rule invented after it closed would make the archive a record of the present rather than of what happened.

**Put the verdict on the counter that already answers the question.** The floor needed a number `queue-size.sh` was already computing. Adding `meets_floor` beside it cost two lines and gave four seams one source; a second counter would have been the same information with a drift surface.

**No audit script was added, deliberately.** Every creation seam now refuses below the floor, so a repo-wide sweep could only ever report zero — and a script nobody re-runs is not a safeguard, it is a file that will one day be wrong without anyone noticing.
