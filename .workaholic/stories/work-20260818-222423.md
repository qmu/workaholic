## 1. Overview

The `[Prepare Release]` tick posts nine times in nine consecutive hours for a single
request. Its `deploy:<digest>` dedup works exactly as specified and still cannot stop the
restatement: `unreleased_count` is in the digest's input and a commit lands on the base
every hour. A second, day-scoped token now bounds the rate without touching the first key.

**Highlights:**

1. Nine posts in nine hours, one request — the refs fix (#503) cured the counts, not the rate.
2. `deploy-day:<day_token>` keys on *what* the tick asks for, not *how much* of it there is.
3. `deploy:<digest>` is unchanged in derivation and format, deliberately.

## 2. Motivation

The complaint was about attention: `[Prepare Release]` had become the channel's dominant
poster and every line asked for the same act. Two causes were tangled in the report and
only one was addressed — the swinging counts were the stale-refs defect closed hours
earlier, leaving the frequency unexamined.

The ticket left itself an escape hatch: if that fix alone brought the rate down, the honest
outcome was to change nothing. Measuring the nine hours after it landed closed the hatch.
Every earlier attempt here moved a heading rather than a gate, twice in two days.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. Bound the release preparation post rate ([716bb318](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/716bb318))

`report-deploy-status.sh` now emits `day_token` — `<Asia/Tokyo day>:<hash of the per-target
needs sets>` — and the `📦` line carries it beside the digest. The gate becomes both token
searches AND'd with `actionable || doubtful`: an unchanged ask is said once a day, a new
kind of ask still the same hour.

## 4. Outcome

One release request now reaches the channel once per Tokyo day instead of once per hour,
while a genuinely new ask still arrives immediately. The post did not get taller, the
heading did not move, and both pre-existing gates are byte-identical.

## 5. Concerns

### The measurement covers nine hours, not the week the ticket asked for

- **Severity:** low
- **Description:** The refs fix landed nine hours before this run, so the week of post counts the acceptance criteria name does not exist yet; the ruling rests on 9 consecutive hours at 9/9 (see [716bb318](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/716bb318) in `.workaholic/tickets/archive/work-20260818-222423/20260818214615-measure-and-bound-the-prepare-release-post-rate.md`)
- **How to Fix:** Re-read `#dev-workaholic` after a week and confirm the `📦` count settled at roughly one per day per distinct ask; the mechanism is deterministic, so a longer window is corroboration rather than a different answer.

### A daily floor can hold a renewed ask for the rest of the day

- **Severity:** low
- **Description:** Keying on `needs` bounds the loss but does not remove it — a release cut at 06:00 that immediately re-accumulates commits produces no further line until the next Tokyo day (see [716bb318](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/716bb318) in `plugins/workaholic/skills/ship/scripts/report-deploy-status.sh`)
- **How to Fix:** If the silence is measured to matter, the smallest next step is a stage-change trigger — the release stage is already derived by `run-note-cadence.sh` — rather than a shorter or configurable window.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- Measuring the channel before writing code closed one of the ticket's own options: the escape hatch ("the refs fix may have been the whole problem") was answered with a table rather than an opinion.
- Refusing the obvious fix and recording why: narrowing the digest was rejected because its derivation had been settled hours earlier, so the churn the ticket complains about was not repeated to fix it.
