# Expire the alert cool-down at the next working day

## Motivation

A red failure alert is deduped by its signature: before posting, read the channel's recent history
and suppress the same signature inside a 24-hour cool-down, replying `↳ still failing` into the
existing alert instead. The rule is right about what it prevents. Its defect was **where the 24
hours were measured from** — the first report, and nothing else.

Measured 2026-08-22 → 23: a unit blocked on an operator ruling at 21:38 JST. Eleven consecutive
ticks reported `blocked`, 2.1 agent-hours were spent, and every one of those escalations was a
`↳ still failing` reply buried in the previous day's thread. Channel level showed nothing for ten
hours, and the developer found the stall by noticing the channel had stopped updating. The
cool-down was working correctly the whole time.

## Outcome

The cool-down expires at the **earlier** of 24 hours after the first report and the **start of the
next working day** — the first hour inside `WORKAHOLIC_WORK_DAYS` at the end of
`WORKAHOLIC_QUIET_HOURS`, in `WORKAHOLIC_QUIET_TZ`. Those three are the check-in gate's own
variables, composed rather than redefined, so the two gates cannot drift apart and no new constant
enters the plugin.

A root re-posted at expiry names **how long** it has been failing (`🔴 Blocked - <signature>,
failing since <time>, <N> ticks`). The first report's shape is unchanged; this second form exists
only at expiry, because by then the duration is the news and a root identical to yesterday's is the
restatement this repository retires posts for.

## Concerns

**One extra post per signature per working day** is the whole cost, and it is intended: by
construction the failure being re-announced is one nobody has acted on. Set against ten hours of a
stalled loop that nobody could see, the trade is not close.

**A signature first reported during the working day is unaffected** — the 24-hour term is still the
earlier one there — so the change is narrower than it reads. The `↳ still failing` reply and its
exemption from rate-limiting, the first-report rule, and the rule that an unreadable history posts
the alert anyway are all untouched, and the skill now says so explicitly rather than leaving their
survival to be inferred.

## Successful Development Patterns

- **Compose the boundary that already exists.** The obvious implementation was a tick-count
  threshold; the thing that actually went wrong was that a person's working day passed with nothing
  to see, and the repository already knew how to compute that day.
- **Say what did not change, in the document that changed.** Four load-bearing clauses sat in the
  same paragraph as the defect; naming their survival in the edit is cheaper than a later reader
  re-deriving whether they were meant to go.
