# Route a stalled unit to a person who is asked by name

## Motivation

A consuming repository's loop stopped for **eleven consecutive hourly ticks**. Every tick ran
correctly, reported `blocked` correctly, and spent agent-hours — 1.6 of them recorded against one
mission that finished at 0/3 acceptance. The only outbound signal in eleven hours was a
mention-less reply threaded into a feedback root written the previous day. Slack notified nobody,
channel level showed nothing, and the developer found the stall by noticing the channel had not
moved in ten hours and asking why.

Every individual mechanism behaved exactly as specified while that was true. `/implement` cannot
ask — no `AskUserQuestion` anywhere, at any step, by design. `🔴 Blocked` and `↳ still failing`
carry no mention token, by design. The maintenance tick's check-in names a person and therefore
notifies them — but it asked only about what its own steps had found, and **no step read the state
of claimed work**. There was no path from *the loop is stuck* to *a person is asked*.

## Outcome

Three tickets, one path, end to end.

**The reading.** `moderate/scripts/step-stalled-units.sh` is step 11 of the tick. It calls the
claim oracle itself (`drive/scripts/list-claims.sh`, a pure read over unmerged remote branches) —
a reader, not a handoff, because a `/implement` run's container is discarded and it writes nothing
into the tree about its own blockers. Per claimed unit it reports the unit, its branch, its holder,
the hours since the branch last moved, and whether it ever reached a pull request.

Two things the pieces did not yet give. The **PR distinction** was computed inside `lib/claims.sh`
but only on the branch where the resumable verdict forked, so `queue_drained` — the commonest state
of a finished-but-unmerged unit — short-circuited before it, and no reader could tell a unit parked
at a pull request from one that never opened any. `claims_has_story` is now evaluated for every row
and emitted as `reported`. The **age** is computed with `date`, not jq's `fromdateiso8601`: the
oracle emits git's `%cI`, which carries the committing machine's offset, and that builtin accepts
only the `Z` form — the step's first run reported five of seven live claims as *unknown age*, which
is precisely the reading a stalled-unit reader must never get wrong.

**The asking.** Every **stale** claim goes to the check-in as a candidate naming its holder, keyed
`stalled-unit:<unit>` so the asked-once ledger asks exactly once, and the question rides the
`🙋 <@U…>` shape that already names a person, sits in the tick's own thread, and carries the
session URL. Every existing gate applies unchanged downstream: five per tick, ten per day, quiet
hours, and the working-week hold.

**The answering.** A question now has three states — `never_asked`, `asked`, `answered` — and the
answer carries the person's **words**, not a flag. `record-answer.sh` writes them into the tick log
through the existing append-only writer, `persist-log.sh` carries them to the base with no branch
and no claim, and `question-state.sh` reads them back. `ask-question.sh` refuses an answered
question under its own reason and hands the words to the caller.

## Journey

The threshold was the mission's one genuine fork, and the ticket required an explicit ruling. Its
three options were a fixed tick count, a working-day boundary, and two-ticks-plus-an-open-decision.
**None was taken.** The claim protocol already owns this decision: `lib/claims.sh` computes `stale`
from `WORKAHOLIC_CLAIM_STALE_HOURS` (default 24) and its header states the meaning in the exact
words the step needs — *a tip older than the threshold says "look at this", not "take it"*. Asking
a person to look **is** that, so the reuse is principled rather than convenient, and it beats each
option on that option's own cost: a fixed count invents an arbitrary constant beside a justified
one; a working-day boundary makes a unit stalling at 09:05 wait nearly a full day when the measured
failure *was* a day of silence, and `stale` has that shape without the cliff; two-ticks-plus-an-
open-decision names one blocker class when a missing credential stalls a unit exactly as hard.

The working-week half of the rejected option is not lost — it lives downstream in the check-in's
own weekend hold, so the threshold never needs to know about days.

## Concerns

**The alert shapes were deliberately not touched.** `🔴 Blocked` and `↳ still failing` still carry
no mention token, and the skill now says why: they are the run's record of an outcome, and the
question is a demand on a person's attention. Two speech acts. Making the record louder is the
direction this repository has retired twice — the failure was never volume, it was that nothing
addressed anybody.

**Nothing parses an answer.** It is a person's prose. What this mission owes is that the words
survive and are found; acting on them stays the next run's judgement. A run that reads an answer
and does the wrong thing with it is a separate problem from a run that never sees one.

**The backlog drains over two ticks, then goes quiet.** This repository currently holds five stale
claims, and the five-per-tick cap plus asked-once means they are asked about across two ticks and
never again. That is the intended shape, but it is worth watching on a repository with many more.

## Successful Development Patterns

- **Reuse a justified constant instead of inventing one.** The threshold question looked like it
  needed a new number and did not; the claim protocol had already answered it, in the same words.
- **Extract the identity derivation the moment a second reader appears.** Three scripts key on a
  question's id now, and an answer filed under one id that never clears a gate reading another is
  exactly the class of defect this mission exists to remove.
- **Ship the reading inert first.** The first ticket changed no observable behaviour, which made
  it provable on its own before anything depended on it.
