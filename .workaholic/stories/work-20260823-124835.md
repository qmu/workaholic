# Make an Open Decision a question to answer, not a ruling

## Motivation

An `## Open Decisions` item was **self-certifying**. One automated seam wrote *this fork cannot be
resolved here*, and the driving run took that sentence as **evidence** rather than as a claim to
check. Nothing anywhere required the run to look.

Both halves were conformant with the text as written, which is why the text moved rather than the
runs being blamed. The failure contract's own words — *"Decide it from the evidence and the stated
intent"*, and *"if you cannot name which of those you are missing, you are not blocked on the
developer; you are declining to decide"* — are satisfied on their face by a session that names an
authority without ever having read a source.

Measured twice. A tick honoured an item whose answer sat **fifty lines further down the very page
the ticket named**, recorded the unit `blocked` without an attempt, and ended `pending`. On a
consuming repository the same shape ran **eleven consecutive ticks** for zero lines of
implementation, 2.1 agent-hours spent, while the operator's own answer sat unread in the same tree.

## Outcome

Both ends of the seam, in the mission's order.

**The driver must look before it may block.** `drive/reference/ticket-workflow.md` §1 now states
that an Open Decision is a question to answer, not a ruling that the question is unanswerable, and
that honouring one as a blocker requires reading the sources the item names — and **the whole of
any page it cites**, not the paragraph the item quotes. `drive/SKILL.md`'s run-report contract and
the `blocked` finish require the sources read and what they said: **a block that cannot name a
source it read is not a block.** `reference/failure-contract.md`'s external-blocker bucket now says
a named authority is **necessary and not sufficient** — naming who must decide is free, so what
qualifies a unit is the evidence of the read.

**The writer must not certify itself.** `create-ticket/reference/ticket-format.md` gives the item
three required parts — the question, the sources consulted and what they said, the fork's sides —
and forbids an item that declares the question unanswerable or instructs a later session not to
decide. `specificate/SKILL.md` and `reference/workflow.md` step 5 require the discovery pass to
have covered the item's own subject, and the whole of any cited page, before an item may be
written, carrying what it found into the item.

## Journey

The two tickets are complementary and neither is sufficient alone: stopping new self-certifying
items does nothing about the ones already in the queue, and requiring the read does nothing about
items that name no source to read. The mission ordered them driver-first because the existing
queue is what is stalling today; the writer floor stops the queue refilling.

The scoping question — how much reading is required — was answered narrowly on purpose: **the
sources the item names, plus the whole of any page it cites**. "Read everything" would be
unfalsifiable and would make every block arguable; "read the paragraph the item quotes" is exactly
what produced the measured failure, because the answer was fifty lines below it.

## Concerns

**This is a prose contract, not a script gate, and that is a real limit.** No mechanical check can
tell a real read from a claimed one. What the change buys is narrower and still worth having: a
blocked report naming no source is visibly non-conformant by the contract's own words, so the
failure is now legible to a reviewer and to the maintenance tick rather than indistinguishable
from correct behaviour.

**The escape hatch stays open, deliberately.** A genuinely unrecommendable fork is still
recordable — with its sources named. Removing the section would have traded one silent failure for
another: a run guessing where it should have asked.

**`/ticket` is untouched**, and should stay so: it resolves the same kind of fork by asking a human
directly, so it never writes a self-certifying item.

## Successful Development Patterns

- **Fix both ends of a seam in one mission.** The defect lived in neither document — it lived in
  the fact that one wrote a claim and the other read it as evidence.
- **Move the text when the runs were conformant.** Both measured failures satisfied the written
  contract; blaming the sessions would have left the contract intact and the failure repeatable.
- **Scope a "read more" requirement to something checkable.** Naming *the sources the item names,
  plus the whole of any cited page* is arguable in a review; "be thorough" is not.
