## 1. Overview

`/specificate` puts the carried-forward `feedback:` refs on the **mission** and its tickets
carry `mission:`. `unit-feedback-stems.sh` never made that hop, so a mission unit resolved
its stems and a batch of that same mission's tickets resolved **nothing** — and a unit with
no stem falls to the notify lookup's case 4 and starts a fresh thread beside the one its own
item is already two replies deep in.

**Highlights:**

1. The hop composes the two existing readers; neither relation gains a second parser.
2. It is archive-aware because `mission_resolve` already is — inherited, not the defect.
3. Direct refs come first, so the old stem set is a **prefix** of the new one.

## 2. Motivation

Measured 2026-08-29 on unit `batch-20260829093639`: two tickets, both naming a mission
carrying two `feedback:` refs, answered `count: 0`. The unit's finish line therefore had
nothing to search on, and the thread that item had been accumulating for the whole mission
got a sibling instead of a reply.

The ticket that queued this named the **archive** as the cause — the archive gate closes a
mission the moment its last ticket is archived, so a follow-up driven after that resolves
through an archived mission by construction. The fixture refuted the hypothesis before a line
was written: a ticket naming an **active** mission answered `count: 0` too. The resolver was
not archive-blind; it made no hop at all.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. Resolve a unit's stems through its mission ([03ba1ded](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/03ba1ded))

After the direct `feedback:` read, each artifact's `mission:` slugs are read through
`mission/scripts/read-relation.sh`, resolved to a `mission.md` through the resolve library's
`mission_resolve`, and that file's own `feedback:` refs appended through the same
`read-feedback-relation.sh` the direct pass used. Deduplication and order are the existing
loop's, unchanged.

Only `mission_resolve` is taken from the resolve library — never `missions_migrate_layout` or
`missions_migrate_status`, which **stage** what they converge. A read-only resolver that
staged would dirty a checkout in order to decide where a Slack post goes.

## 4. Outcome

A batch unit whose tickets carry no refs of their own now posts its finish line into the
thread its mission's item already owns, whether that mission is active or archived. A unit
that already resolved stems is byte-identical: the direct refs still sort first, so the
first-sorting-stem rule sees exactly what it saw.

## 5. Concerns

### A mission slug this checkout has never had resolves to nothing, silently

- **Severity:** low
- **Description:** An unresolvable `mission:` slug leaves the hop empty for that artifact and is not an error (see [03ba1ded](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/03ba1ded) in `plugins/workaholic/skills/drive/scripts/unit-feedback-stems.sh`)
- **How to Fix:** Accepted and asserted. An empty result has always been an answer here rather than a failure — the caller keys such a unit on `unit:<unit-id>` instead — and a shallow or partial checkout naming a mission it does not carry is an ordinary condition, not a defect to escalate on.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- Writing the fixture for the ticket's stated cause **before** the repair falsified the
  hypothesis in one run: the active-mission case answered `count: 0` too, which turned an
  archive-awareness patch into the missing hop it actually was.
- Composing the two single readers rather than reading either field directly kept the
  one-parser-per-relation rule intact through a change whose whole subject is a second
  relation.

## 7. Notes

`attributed-work.sh` has always needed a `via_mission:<slug>` hop for exactly this reason.
This resolver is the second reader to need it, and it reaches it by composition rather than
by a shared walker — the two answer different questions (which work a direction owns; where a
post belongs) and share only the two readers underneath.
