# Make a direction's lifecycle a declared stage

## 1. Overview

Every reading in the direction layer was **derived** — `pace`, `overdue`, `expiring`,
`dormant`, `quiescent`, and the one lifecycle answer composed from them — so a direction's
phase read as *converged or not* and the loop could only ever infer it. The operator asked for
a **declared** phase in their own words: 進行中 (not yet cut over), 改良中 (cut over, still
improving), 観察中 (settled; reactive only).

**Highlights:**

1. `stage:` is a field on the artifact with **one** writer set (unchanged at three), **one**
   reader resolving its absent default, and **no migration** — absent means 進行中
2. 観察中 is the **first declared gate** on `/propose`'s ladder of derived ones; it stops
   origination and nothing else, and inbound work still reaches a settled direction
3. 改良中 joins the eligible **sort** and nothing else — an order, never a gate
4. Two transition questions say what the layer could only say backwards before: *can this cut
   over* and *has this settled*

## 2. Motivation

The layer answered *is this direction in trouble* four ways and *has it arrived* once, all of
it computed from evidence. None of it could answer *where does the loop's energy belong*,
because that is a judgement the operator makes and no reading can reach — a feature toggle no
script can see, a decision that improving is finished. Directions improve as a blend, and the
stage is what says which of several concurrent directions can absorb a proposal now.

The constraint that shaped every ticket: a stage change is an **operator-auditable act, never a
machine's silent reclassification**. That rule already had a shape here — `amend.sh`, reached
only from an announcement matched by explicit slug, onto a pull request the publish seam itself
refuses to auto-merge — so the stage became that writer's fourth revisable part rather than
acquiring machinery of its own.

## 3. Changes

1. **The field.** `stage:` from the closed set, in the operator's characters, kept verbatim.
   `create.sh` takes `--stage` and refuses `bad_stage` with nothing written; `read.sh` resolves
   an absent field to 進行中 **there and nowhere else**; `list.sh` composes that reader rather
   than parsing the field again; `validate-strategy.sh` checks the closed set and never
   requires presence. Every committed strategy stays valid and byte-identical — there is no
   migration.

2. **The move.** `--stage` joins `amend.sh`'s revisable parts, reusing `create.sh`'s
   `bad_stage` verbatim so one artifact never grows two names for one refusal. A move onto a
   direction carrying no line **inserts** it where `create.sh` puts it; the dated `## Schedule`
   line names the **move** (`進行中 → 改良中`) reading the previous value through `read.sh`.
   `/specificate` step 9d carries what the ask states and judges none of it. **No fourth
   writer, no new route, no new refusal vocabulary.**

3. **The reading.** `stage` rides every survey row (eligible **and** refused — the refused case
   is the point, since a settled direction is normally refused) and every `direction-state.sh`
   row, beside `target_date` and `landed`. A **sixth `state` value** was refused for the reason
   `overdue` was kept out of `pace`: one field answering two questions is how the two drift.

4. **The gate.** A 観察中 direction is refused **`observing`**, placed after `not_active` and
   `not_mine` (a closed or foreign direction is not this repository's question) and before
   `past_target_date` (lateness on a settled direction is not a failure). It stops
   **origination only**: the refused row keeps every reading, and an inbound ask still becomes
   an issue, still reaches `/specificate`, and still lands as work carrying that direction's
   refs.

5. **The order.** 改良中 is the first component of the existing sort key, with late-first and
   nearest-date unchanged beneath it. No weight, no score, no tunable constant.

6. **The renders.** The morning digest, the bare `/mission` roadmap and every `direction-*`
   question heading name the declared stage; an unresolved one renders as **unreadable**, never
   as the default.

7. **The questions.** `direction-cutover:<slug>` and `direction-settled:<slug>`.

8. **The drill.** `verify-stage`, hermetic, 16 load-bearing rows, breaker proved able to fail.

## 4. Outcome

An operator can declare where a direction stands, in their own vocabulary, and the loop acts on
it: it stops originating for a settled direction, spends its proposing attention on the one
declared able to absorb it, shows the phase wherever directions are read, and asks — once — when
the evidence suggests a boundary was crossed. Nothing infers a stage, nothing moves one, and a
repository that never adopts the vocabulary behaves exactly as it did.

## 5. Concerns

1. **The transition questions refine an existing question rather than adding one, and the
   ticket's literal steps could not be satisfied.** `direction-state.sh` projects `quiescent` to
   `arrived` and `dormant` to `dormant` in a fixed precedence, so a question added *beside*
   those would double-ask one direction or never fire; and the ticket's own 2→3 terms were
   contradictory (`quiescent` requires landed work, which the same sentence then excludes). The
   fork was resolved from the ticket's Overview and the mission's Experience and is recorded in
   the step header, `reference/workflow.md` and the ticket's Final Report rather than decided
   silently. **The stated cost**: a direction already asked `direction-arrived` may be asked
   `direction-cutover` once — one extra question, ever, and the better-aimed one.

2. **`stage_declared` is a new reader field.** Keying the refinement on the *resolved* stage
   made the loop assert a declaration nobody had made ("still declared 進行中" of an unstaged
   direction) and silently rewrote the question for every direction in every existing
   repository — caught by a pre-existing test. `read.sh` now says which of the two answers a
   caller holds. It is a **reader** field, not an artifact field, nothing gates on it, and the
   absent default still has exactly one derivation; but it is a term four scripts now carry.

3. **A swallowed jq error reads as a quiet tick.** `step-direction-health.sh` ends its subject
   composition with `jq -c '…' 2>/dev/null || echo '[]'`, so a jq **compile** error is discarded
   and the step reports `status: ok` with `0 to ask`. Measured live during this mission: a tick
   that found two directions needing a person, could not compile the expression naming them,
   and called itself healthy. It is the **shape** rather than the file, so it is queued as
   `20260829231500-stop-a-swallowed-jq-error-reading-as-a-quiet-tick.md` rather than patched
   here — which is why this mission's queue is **not** drained.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- **An existing test failing is the cheapest evidence about blast radius.**
  `direction-arrived:dir1` becoming `direction-cutover:dir1` in a fixture that never mentioned
  a stage was the entire argument for keying the refinement on a declared value — delivered in
  one line, before any reasoning about it.

- **A byte-identity pin across a value set becomes a liability when one member is meant to
  differ.** Ticket 3's "the stage gates nothing" broke the moment ticket 4's gate landed.
  Narrowing it and naming 観察中 as the one deliberate exception kept the guarantee precise;
  exempting the value silently would have left a pin that no longer said anything.

- **Write the breaker against the behaviour, then run it.** The stage drill's breaker had its
  condition inverted and still reported "did not break" while the seam was demonstrably broken:
  wiring the gate at `dormant` silences *every* direction in that fixture, so the damage
  appears as an empty `selected` rather than as the declared one escaping.

- **A guard for a class, not an instance.** Two scripts broke on an apostrophe inside a comment
  in a single-quoted jq program — a total failure invisible to every other test, whose error
  points hundreds of lines from the cause. `every shipped shell script parses` was added to the
  suite and proved able to fail by re-introducing the defect.
