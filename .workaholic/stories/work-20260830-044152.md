## 1. Overview

One `mergeability` reading fed two candidate sets that disagreed. `/moderate`'s `catchup-blocked`
step **asks** the claim holder about a `content` conflict on a `report_undelivered` **or** a
`queue_drained` claim; `/implement` **acted** only on `report_undelivered`. So a `queue_drained`
claim — finished, pushed, waiting on a person — was asked about once its conflict had **hardened**
and never acted on while it was still machine-settleable: the loop waiting for its own finished
work to become somebody's job.

**Highlights:**

1. `drive/scripts/list-catchable-claims.sh` widens the **caller**, composing `list-claims.sh` and
   re-deriving nothing; `catch-up-claim.sh` is untouched by the widening itself.
2. One bound is genuinely new and belongs to the widening: **`pull_request_reviewed`**, with a
   three-valued `reviews_unreadable:<reason>` that never falls through as *nobody has reviewed*.
3. *When a bounded act may read a judgement* is written once in `drive/reference/claims.md`, its
   consumers enumerated, pinned **both ways** and proved able to fail in both directions.

## 2. Motivation

`catch-up-claim.sh` never carried a delivery-verdict gate of its own. The only reason it never
saw a `queue_drained` claim is **which loop called it** — `/implement`'s `undelivered[]` walk,
whose entries are by definition units a *transport* refused. The catch-up's whole subject is
whether the *base* still accepts a branch, which is a different question from whether a merge
was refused, and the two had been welded together by a caller.

Measured live on this repository while the mission was written: one reported claim had been
`mechanical` for four days with nothing acting on it, and another had already hardened to
`content` and stood for twelve. The first was repairable by a machine on any of those four days.
The reader written here, run against the live tree before a line of the caller existed, named
exactly that unit — `deploy-the-docs-site-on-merge-to-main`, `queue_drained`, `mechanical` — which
is the mission's own premise confirmed rather than assumed.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. The rule the widening is bounded by, written before it was exercised

`claims.md` says a consumer may **act** on a proof and may only **report** or **ask about** a
judgement — and every `mergeability` value is classified there as a judgement. Yet
`catch-up-claim.sh` reads `mechanical` and acts on it. Discovery found the exception already
half-written: the `mechanical` row itself carried it, as a fact about **one word**.

That is correct and it cannot govern a **fourth** act somebody adds next month. So the general
rule now has one home, with four clauses that are all load-bearing: an act may read a judgement
only when it **re-derives that judgement at the moment of the act**, is **idempotent**, is
**reversible**, and **refuses every bound by its own word**. The `mechanical` row was narrowed to
point at it rather than carrying a second copy.

Two things the rule explicitly does **not** reach, because the tempting reading is that a bound
act may take anything: a judgement that is the **absence** of a reading (`unanswerable`,
`identity_unresolved`, `shallow_history`) is never actable however bounded — clause 1 cannot even
be satisfied, since the re-derivation answers nothing either — and a judgement whose next step is
a **person's judgement** stays a person's. `content` stays refused, `awaiting_verification` stays
reported, `stale` stays reported and never acted on.

The pin runs **both ways**, and one direction alone would be half a rule: a script that both
reads a judgement-emitting reader and carries an acting call site must appear in the table
(an unenumerated act has no bound at all), and a consumer named there that stops re-deriving
fails (an enumerated act trusting a handed-in reading has lost the clause that makes the
exception safe). Both were broken on purpose and each turned its own rows red before being
reverted.

### 3-2. The reader, and the term it deliberately does not look up

`list-catchable-claims.sh` answers this identity's **reported** claims (`report_undelivered` or
`queue_drained`) whose `mergeability` is `mechanical`, resolved through `lib/claims.sh`'s
**live-row rule**. It composes `list-claims.sh` — one walk of the refs — and re-derives nothing:
not `mergeability`, not the resume reason, not the refs walk.

`clean` is deliberately **not** a candidate. There is nothing to catch up and the writer would
answer `already_current` after attaching a worktree, so filtering here is what keeps the steady
state — every reported claim current with the base — costing a run nothing rather than a worktree
per claim per tick.

The **open-pull-request** term is read off the row and costs no lookup, which the ticket asked to
be measured rather than assumed. Both admitted verdicts already *mean* "at an open pull request"
in the oracle's own definitions. A REST call per candidate here would derive a second time what
the verdict already carries, duplicate the lookup the writer makes for its own review bound, and
make a pure offline read need a credential.

A degraded scan yields no candidates, its reason and **null** counts — never a bare empty set,
which is byte-identical to a healthy quiet run and means the opposite.

### 3-3. The one bound the widening adds

An `undelivered` unit's pull request was refused by a **transport**: nobody is looking at it. A
`queue_drained` unit's may be one a person is **mid-review** on, and a push resets an approval.
So `catch-up-claim.sh` gains `pull_request_reviewed`, refusing with the branch byte-identical and
exit 0, placed after every offline bound and before the first act so a refusal short-circuits with
no worktree attached and no network call spent on a question already answered locally.

What counts as a person's attention is **decided in the script's header** rather than inherited
from the seam: the reviews endpoint returns only *submitted* reviews, so presence is submission;
`APPROVED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `COMMENTED` and `DISMISSED` all count, because the safer reading
of an ambiguous seam is that somebody looked; and a **bot's** review is not a person's, since a
review bot comments on every pull request the loop opens and counting it would refuse the whole
widening.

The lookup is **three-valued** for the merged-pull-request lookup's own reason: every way of
failing to ask answers `reviews_unreadable:<reason>` rather than falling through, because a wrong
*nobody has reviewed* pushes over somebody's approval while a wrong refusal delays a unit by an
hour.

### 3-4. The caller, the report, and the filter that turned out not to exist

Both entry points walk the reader beside the `undelivered[]` loop, one run per candidate, never
batched and never retried inside the run. **A unit in both sets is caught up once** — the
`undelivered[]` loop takes it, because that loop must catch up *before* its retry, and its outcome
is reported there. `/drive` walks it identically: the act asks nothing, needs no ruling, and
refuses the contested case.

Each candidate reports one of `caught_up` / `already_current` / `catch_up_refused: <word>`,
**verbatim** and in the **same three words** the `undelivered[]` path already uses. Beside them
the report now names each **reported** claim's `mergeability`, off the row the oracle already
renders, so a decay from `mechanical` to `content` is visible the hour it happens rather than only
once a question fires. Both are **evidence, never a verdict**: no gate, sort, claim, route,
demotion or token reads either.

The last ticket asked whether `/moderate`'s `catchup-blocked` step must now filter out a unit the
run repaired. **Measured: it must not**, and the honest outcome was a recorded finding and no
code. The two candidate sets are **disjoint by construction** — that step's candidates are
`content` and everything the run may catch up is `mechanical` — and a caught-up branch contains
the base and reads `clean` at the next read anyway. The step is byte-identical; the reasoning
lives in its header so nobody re-opens it and adds a filter to have added one.

### 3-5. The drill

`verify-catch-up` gained five rows over the **same** fixture (a `queue_drained` mechanical claim
offered and caught up, a reviewed pull request refused with the branch byte-identical, a bounded
offer, a null-count degraded read) and a **second breaker**, written against the behaviour: wire
the candidate reader back at the delivery verdict alone and `batch-drained` disappears from the
offer while every other row stays green. Proved red, then reverted. Asserting a return shape would
have survived exactly that narrowing.

The stub was extended rather than the drill reaching the network — a drill that needs a credential
is classified out of the CI set and stops proving anything on merge. `catch_up_mechanical_delivered`
now runs against a pull request carrying a **bot's** review, so that half of the bound is drilled
by the row that was already there.

## 4. Concerns

- **The reader offers a scan-held unit and the writer refuses it, once per tick.** A
  `merge_not_attempted: <tier>` unit routes to `queue_drained` and is `mechanical`, so it is a
  candidate by the ticket's own acceptance wording, and `catch-up-claim.sh` answers
  `scan_held:<tier>` before attaching a worktree. That is the documented gate working and it costs
  nothing but a report line, so no unasked-for filter was added to the reader; it is recorded here
  because the line will recur hourly while such a unit exists. (severity: low)

- **The heartbeat lapsed during this branch's own drive and a concurrent session took the claim
  over.** The implementation ran ~65 minutes without a beat, so `[Implement]`'s next tick read the
  claim as `heartbeat_lapsed` and pushed a `Resume a PR-unit` commit at 05:41 UTC. The work was
  landed by **merging** the takeover — never a rebase or a force-push — so nothing was lost and
  the holder's checkout stays valid. It is the protocol behaving exactly as designed against a
  driving run that did not do its part of it. (severity: low)

## 5. Successful Development Patterns

- **Measuring before writing the filter.** The last ticket's own Considerations sanctioned "no
  code and a recorded finding" as an outcome, and that is what it turned out to be — the sets are
  disjoint by construction. Writing a filter would have added a mechanism whose only effect was
  to look diligent.
- **Running the new reader against the live tree before the caller existed.** It named the exact
  unit the mission's Goal describes, which turned the premise from a claim into a measurement.
- **Breaking the pin on purpose, in both directions.** The bounded-act rule is prose, and prose
  cannot enforce itself; each direction was introduced, shown red on its own rows, and reverted.
