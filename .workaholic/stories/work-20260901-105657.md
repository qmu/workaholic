## 1. Overview

A takeover of a lapsed claim found all three of its queued tickets already blocked on one
operator ruling. The run re-verified that block by reading the sources rather than re-probing the
transport, narrowed what the ruling actually has to decide, and moved the one piece of driveable
work out from underneath it.

**Highlights:**

1. Two of the mechanism ticket's three proposed rulings are already answered in `claims.md`; only
   the re-scope-or-abandon fork is genuinely open.
2. The probe-branch cleanup was bound to a blocked ticket, so nothing in the queue named it as
   work — it is now its own ticket.
3. No ref was pushed to origin: re-probing is what left the residue in the first place.

## 2. Motivation

The mission's first acceptance item asks the claim to contend for one ref per unit. Four
independent readings have now established that this container's transport refuses ref creation
everywhere except `refs/heads/*` and refuses the delete there, so the named mechanism cannot be
built here — and the tickets that depend on it are blocked in turn. Each hourly tick was
re-claiming the unit and stopping at the same place, and the cost of that was not only the tick:
the one genuinely actionable item the measurements produced, two undeletable branches standing on
origin, was recorded as a paragraph inside the blocked ticket, where nothing would ever reach it.

The temptation on a block this well-documented is to confirm it again the same way it was
confirmed before. That is what the ticket's own warning forbids, and it is why the residue exists.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. Re-verify the block by reading, and narrow the ruling

The mechanism ticket offers three rulings. `claims.md` already refuses the CI-executor option by
name — an arbitration must be decided synchronously, in the container, before the run drives
anything — and already records that the named mechanism reaches the mission grain but not the
batch grain, where `claim.sh` mints the unit id inside the claim act itself. That second half is
independent of the transport, so even an environment where every namespace were writable would
not deliver the acceptance item as written. What is left for the operator is the re-scope or the
abandon, and both assert intent.

### 3-2. Lift the probe-branch cleanup into its own ticket

`20260901123000-delete-the-branches-the-transport-probes-left-on-origin.md` names the two refs,
confirms them present, and bounds the repair: delete what you were handed, never what a job
discovered, and do not widen `delete-retired-claim-branch.sh`, whose refusal set is what makes it
safe. The mechanism ticket's *Residue* paragraph now points at it and keeps the measurements.

## 4. Outcome

No acceptance item moved, and all three of the mission's queued tickets stay **blocked** on the
same operator ruling — re-scope the first acceptance item to a mechanism this environment can
express, or abandon it on the bounded-later repair that already landed. Both assert intent, so an
unattended run may not choose either. One driveable ticket was added to the queue for a later run.

The block was re-verified by **reading** its sources, never by re-measuring them: `claims.md`'s
