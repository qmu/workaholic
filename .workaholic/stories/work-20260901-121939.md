## 1. Overview

The cleanup ticket queued an hour earlier asked where the delete of two undeletable probe
branches should run. This run answered it — a person, not a workflow — and wrote the two exact
commands into the ticket. No capability was added and no ref was pushed.

**Highlights:**

1. The `workflow_dispatch` job the ticket sketched was **not** built: two branches, once, do not
   earn a standing `contents: write` grant to delete a branch.
2. It would not have helped anyway — this container cannot dispatch a workflow either, so the
   dispatch would be a person's act exactly as the push is.
3. The ticket stays queued: writing the instruction down is not the same as the refs being gone,
   and the ticket says so itself.

## 2. Motivation

Two probe branches have stood on origin since 2026-08-31 and 2026-08-28, left by the transport
measurements that established what the claim can contend for. Nothing in the loop can delete
them: `refs/heads/*` is the one namespace this container may create in, and its delete is refused
403 by both sanctioned transports.

The tempting repair is the one the repository has a precedent for — move the refused write to CI,
as `claim-retirement.yml` does. The precedent does not transfer. That job exists because a
**recurring, automated** act is refused in the container; this is a one-off cleanup of two names,
and building a job for it would leave a permanent branch-deleting capability behind to close a
problem that closes itself once the branches are gone.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. Answer step 2 and state the act

The ticket's Drive Findings now record the decision with its bound, the reasoning that would
invert it (dozens of branches rather than two), and the two `git push origin --delete` commands to
run from a checkout with ordinary push rights — plus the one command that confirms neither commit
carries work before it is deleted.

No acceptance item moved and no ticket was archived. The mission's three mechanism tickets stay

## 8. Notes

The decision is reversible in one direction only in practice: not building the job costs nothing
if it later turns out to be wanted, while a standing delete capability is awkward to withdraw once
something depends on it.
