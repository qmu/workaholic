## 1. Overview

The `/moderate` tick log moved off `main` on 2026-09-01 (issue #782) — a change worth ~50 commits a
day on the development target. Its eight tickets stayed queued while the mechanism landed through
another branch, so this drive verified each one against the checkout and found exactly one thing
genuinely broken: **the drill that guards the whole move had never once run.**

**Highlights:**

1. `verify-log-branch` looked for `log-ref.sh` and `ensure-log-ref.sh` under `moderate/scripts`,
   where they have never lived — the ref is a fact about the **repository**, so both are
   `gather/scripts`'. It exited `log_branch_seam_unreadable` on every invocation since it was
   written, which `verify-all` reports as **`skipped`**, not `fail`.
2. Repaired, it passes 8 load rows and its breaker, and the drill set's skip count drops from 3 to
   2 — the first time either property of this mission has been proved by anything.
3. Documented in `docs/loop-drill-runbook.md` §5v: an operator-procedure line and a blame row per
   assertion, so a red row sends a reader to the file rather than to the drill's body.

## 2. Motivation

A drill that cannot find its seam does not go red. It exits its seam-check code, `verify-all`
prints `skipped:<reason>` beside the `needs_server` rows that are skipped legitimately, and the
aggregate still exits zero. So the guard for a mission whose whole point is *this must not silently
come back* was itself silently absent — the same failure shape, one level up.

The rest of the mission was already sound and is verified rather than rewritten: the ref, the
publisher, the readers, the record half, the persist count and the ruling on the history left on
`main`.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. The drill's seam paths, named by directory

`_gth` for the ref's derivation (`gather/scripts`), `_mod` for the tick's own scripts
(`moderate/scripts`), with the split's reason in the drill's header so a future move of either is
visible rather than silent. Nothing about what the drill asserts changed.

Now executing: `log_ref_is_not_the_base`, `log_reaches_its_branch`, `log_never_reaches_main`,
`log_day_file_on_the_branch`, `fresh_clone_carries_no_log`, `hydrate_restores_the_memory`,
`log_branch_drill_touches_nothing`, and the breaker `log_ref_may_not_be_the_base`.

### 3-2. The runbook, §5v

The drill had a register row and nothing else — no procedure line, no blame table. Both are written
now, including why the two properties share one drill: they are observed on one fixture and one
persist, the negative one being `git rev-parse refs/heads/main` before and after the very call the
positive one reads.

### 3-3. What was verified, not rewritten

- **The ref**: `workaholic-log`, derived once in `gather/scripts/log-ref.sh`, matching neither
  `work-*` nor `release/*`; `refs/claims/*` was ruled out by a measured 403, not by taste.
- **The publisher**: the publish tree carried onto a different target, so the union by
  `(tick, step)` needed no re-proof; the bounded retry and the `degraded` report survive.
- **The readers**: `log-read.sh` is still the only parser. The move changed the log's *transport*,
  not its path in the checkout, so hydrate puts the bytes where all six readers already looked.
- **The records**: `--record` carries them to `main` in their own commit, named one by one, each
  reported `carried` / `already_on_base` / `missing` / `unreadable` / `unlanded`.
- **The persists**: all three kept and justified by name. The count was never the defect — the
  destination was.
- **The history**: left on `main`. The area stays registered **permissively**, so existing day
  files are legal history while the git-ignore blocks new writes.

## 5. Concerns

### A drill can be dead for its whole life without any surface going red

- **Severity:** moderate
- **Description:** `skipped:<reason>` is the same verdict for *this needs a server* and *this drill
  cannot find its own scripts*. The first is a classification, the second is a defect, and
  `verify-all` exits zero over both. This drill was dead from the day it was written.
- **How to Fix:** Not fixed here — the ticket in hand was the drill, not the aggregate's
  vocabulary. Worth a rule that a `skipped` naming a **path** is a failure while `needs_server`
  and `unclassified` keep their current standing, and worth reading the current skip list as a
  to-do rather than as a legend.

### The mission's mechanism landed on a branch that was not its own

- **Severity:** low
- **Description:** Issue #782 put the whole move on `main` while these eight tickets stayed queued,
  so the mission read `0/3` with a full queue and was offered to every survey.
- **How to Fix:** `/moderate`'s `unrecorded-missions` step is the reader for this shape. This drive
  resolves this instance by driving and archiving the tickets, which converges the arithmetic.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

Verifying against the checkout is a legitimate outcome of driving a ticket — but it is only
legitimate when the verification is real enough to find what is broken. Seven of these eight
verified clean; the eighth found a drill that had never executed, which no amount of reading the
mission's documentation would have surfaced.
