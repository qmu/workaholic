## 1. Overview

The tick's allocation was a constant and the loop's state was not, so the bottleneck never got
capacity and a runner with nothing to do was walked anyway. Measured over two hours: 54 tickets
across 8 active missions, **one** `implement` runner by construction — the concurrency rule forbade
a second — about seven hours of serial queue; and `propose`'s strategy half produced no proposal on
any run, each time re-deriving a gate only `implement` could clear.

**Highlights:**

1. Two readers the tick never had: how much work is **independently claimable**, and what the
   **machine** can carry. Neither ever answers a plausible number for a reading it could not make.
2. `implement` fans out to a declared bound. **No runner is handed a unit** — each surveys and
   claims for itself, and the claim arbiter settles the race.
3. The **ingest** half is split from the strategy half and runs on the tick's own capture, so an
   ask no longer waits up to fifteen minutes for a clock.
4. Every allocation is reported, and a tick that chose to watch says so as a **decision**.

## 2. Motivation

The concurrency rule — one agent per name — was written when every loop had exactly one job, and it
made adding capacity not merely un-attempted but forbidden. The safety needed to relax it had
already shipped for a different reason: since 2026-09-02 `claim.sh` wins one ref per **claimed
artifact** before it creates anything, so two runners that survey together cannot both take one
unit and the loser refuses `claim_race_lost` holding nothing. The fan-out is the first consumer of
that argument, not a new one.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. `claimable-units.sh` — how much work there is

Composes `plan-units.sh`, the executor's own survey, and derives nothing of its own: no second
walker, no count of `todo/` files, no network read. All loose backlog counts as **one** unit,
because the batch partition is a judgement made at §2 of the Unified Run — under-counting spawns
fewer runners than the queue could carry, over-counting spawns runners that lose a claim race for
nothing. Only `heartbeat_lapsed` and `report_incomplete` count among resumables.

**Cost, measured and stated rather than worked around**: `plan-units.sh` takes **68–73 seconds** on
this machine, three consecutive warm runs — roughly a quarter of a five-minute tick. A cheaper
number the executor would then refuse is not a saving.

### 3-2. `read-machine-load.sh` — what the machine can carry

A POSIX `sh` pure read: `nproc` (with `getconf` as fallback) and the first field of
`/proc/loadavg`. It answers `cores` / `load1` / `load_per_core`, opens no network connection,
writes nothing, and exits 0 in every case. **No consumer reads it** — this ticket changes no
decision, so it lands and is wrong about nothing.

It answers **`null`, never `0`**: a zero load reads as an idle machine, the one answer that would
make a consumer fan out hardest at exactly the moment it must not.

### 3-3. The fan-out, the split, the deferral

`implement` spawns `min(WORKAHOLIC_IMPLEMENT_FANOUT, claimable, bound − running)`, each runner
under its own name; **absent means 1**, `bad_fanout` holds nothing, only `running` runners count
so a fan-out does not compound across ticks, and a degraded claimable reading yields **no** fan-out
with the reader's own word reported.

`propose` and `ingest` are separate loops: the strategy judgement keeps its cadence, the ingest is
spawned whenever §1 filed an issue this tick — keyed on the tick's own act and nothing else — and
still runs on the cadence for an ask filed directly on GitHub. When both fire, the strategy
judgement is spawned first.

The strategy half is **deferred** while its own last reported refusal was `work_waiting`, read from
the tick log rather than re-derived; lifted the moment an `implement` run lands a unit; capped at
`WORKAHOLIC_PROPOSE_DEFER_MAX` (default 3); and never deferred on a refusal only a person clears.

## 5. Concerns

- **The fan-out is inert until the operator declares the bound.** `WORKAHOLIC_IMPLEMENT_FANOUT`
  lives in `.claude/settings.json`, which an unattended run cannot edit; absent means 1, so this
  branch changes the loop's behaviour in no way until that line exists. That is the safety
  property, and it is also the reason the mission is not finished. Severity: medium.
- **The machine bound has a reader and no consumer.** `read-machine-load.sh` lands with nothing
  reading it, by its own ticket's design. Until `refuse-a-fan-out-the-machine-cannot-carry` lands,
  a declared fan-out is bounded by the queue and the number, and not by the machine — which is the
  state measured at loadavg `7.99` on four cores. Severity: medium.
- **The claimable reading costs ~70s of a 300s tick.** Reported as a finding, per its ticket. If
  the fan-out makes ticks longer, this is the first place to look. Severity: low.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- **Measure the constraint before designing around it.** The survey's cost was timed three times
  rather than assumed, and the answer (~70s) is a real quarter of the tick — worth stating as a
  finding rather than routing around with a lighter count the executor would refuse.
- **A reading that failed must not answer a plausible number.** Both readers answer `null` with a
  named reason. For the machine that is load-bearing in the strongest sense: `0` reads as idle,
  which is the one answer that would make the consumer fan out hardest exactly when it must not.
- **Read a previous run's reported answer, not its gate.** The deferral reaches the tick log — the
  same seam `blocked-tick` and the change diff already use — which is what keeps it from being the
  change-detector the command body refuses by name.
- **A brake on the routine that originates work needs a ceiling.** This loop has twice stopped
  silently that way, so the cap is the load-bearing half of the deferral, not a tuning knob.
