## 1. Overview

The operator restated the grain: a mission is the **mid-term container** between a strategy and a
ticket, with room to plan tickets across a period. The corpus did not match it, and two mechanisms
made that — **one mission per inbound ask**, and **a scale written as a count to hit**.

**Highlights:**

1. The grain is stated **once**, in `rules/workaholic.md`, and every other surface cites it. No
   surface names a ticket count as the criterion any more.
2. The two-ticket floor runs at **every** seam that publishes a mission with its ticket set —
   including `/mission`'s **replan**, which is the seam a below-floor mission actually reaches.
3. The loop can **see its own corpus** for the first time: one pass, a bucketed distribution on the
   morning root, and an advisory naming what the forward-only floor left behind.

## 2. Motivation

The measurements are the argument, and each was checked before anything was built for it.

Over **126 missions and 1335 tickets** in this repository, read by the new script rather than by
hand: **53 at two or three tickets**, 30 at four to six, 30 at exactly seven or eight, 12 at nine
or more, and **one below the floor** — which rule 1 says cannot exist. The old wording, *roughly
7–8 tickets, the ruled scale*, is visible in that distribution as a spike; the ask's own hand count
put 52% at exactly seven or eight.

Separately, the ingest side: eight asks arrived from the channel in one hour, seven became
missions, forty-eight tickets between them, inside twenty-one minutes. Row 1 of `/specificate`'s
form precedence read on **decomposition alone**, which is *one mission per inbound ask*.

Rule 1 held at exactly one seam. `check-floor.sh` ran at `/specificate`'s publish seam and
`open-proposal.sh` floored a *proposal*; `/mission`'s creation path, its replan, and any
hand-written mission reached no floor at all.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. The rule's home (`rules/workaholic.md`, *What a Mission Must Be Able to Hold*)

The container definition in the operator's own words, **rule 1** as the mechanical floor, **rule 2**
as a stated position — *many two- and three-ticket missions is a defect the loop refuses, not a
size it may choose* — the criterion as one arguable question, and the measurement, so the next
reader argues with the evidence rather than the wording. It says explicitly that rule 2 must never
become a second threshold: the moment a number is written as the criterion, the criterion is the
number.

`CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`, `commands/propose.md`, `propose/SKILL.md`, `specificate/SKILL.md` and
`open-proposal.sh`'s header now **cite** it. A count survives only as an observation about typical
size.

### 3-2. The floor, at every seam

`mission/SKILL.md` carries the enumeration as a table — `/specificate`'s publish seam,
`/mission`'s Creation Interrogation, `/mission`'s **replan**, and `open-proposal.sh`'s proposal-body
floor — with the two seams that deliberately hold no call (`create.sh`, `close.sh`) and why.
`command-flows.md`'s replan step 5 gained the call: a `carried` successor arrives with **zero**
tickets and replan is the sanctioned path that fleshes it out, so it is where a below-floor mission
is actually born.

`validate-mission.sh` is **not** a seam, and its header now says why — the hook fires at the write
of `mission.md`, before any ticket exists, so a count there would refuse every legitimate mission
on its first write. That was the next reader's obvious move and it is headed off in the file.

### 3-3. The corpus reader (`mission/scripts/size-distribution.sh`)

One pass over the ticket tree, each ticket's `mission:` relation read through `read-relation.sh` —
still the single reader of that many-valued field — tallied by slug and bucketed
`0 / 1 / 2-3 / 4-6 / 7-8 / 9+`, with `below_floor` beside it. The floor is **read** from
`queue-size.sh`'s own derivation against an empty root, never spelled.

`/moderate`'s `strategy-digest` step carries it on the JST-morning root — **once a day, never
hourly**, on the same reasoning that retired `📦 Release Preparation`. It gates nothing and names
no slug.

### 3-4. The audit's advisory

`layout-doctor.sh` names each below-floor mission as an **advisory**, composing the reader above.
`conforming` stays true and the `Validate Plugins` merge gate is untouched — the duplicate-slug
pair's own precedent, cited in the code beside the new block. It deletes nothing, closes nothing
and chooses nothing.

### 3-5. Row 1's second term

`/specificate`'s row 1 now requires **both** terms: the ask decomposes into two or more units
**and** there is a mid-term plan to hold. An ask that decomposes with no such plan takes the next
row it fits, and the judgement is reported by name beside `precedence:<form>` so a reader can
disagree with it. Every existing floor stays over the top.

## 4. Outcome

A mission is emitted because there is a mid-term plan to hold, not because an ask arrived. One
below two tickets cannot be written at any seam. The scale is stated as what the container must
hold, and the loop can measure whether that is working.

## 5. Concerns

- **Rule 2 is prose and nothing mechanical checks it.** What the suite pins is that no surface
  carries the old count-as-criterion wording, that rule 1 stayed a gate, and that rule 2 did not
  become a numeric one. Whether a given ask really holds a mid-term plan is a judgement, by
  design.
- **The hand count and the machine reading disagree by one ticket.** The ask said one mission at a
  *single* ticket; the reader says one at **zero**. Both are below the floor and the advisory names
  it either way, but the reading composed from the relation reader is the number to argue with from
  here.
- **Composing `queue-size.sh` per mission was rejected by measurement, not taste**: it walks the
  whole ticket tree per answer — 126 × 1335 reads, over ten minutes in an audit CI runs on every
  merge. One pass is 7 seconds. The rejection is recorded in both script headers so it is not
  re-proposed blind.
- **No cap was placed on the ingest path**, deliberately and by name: `WORKAHOLIC_WIP_LIMIT` holds
  origination only, and a limit that swallowed the operator's own instructions on a busy day would
  be a worse defect than the one it cures.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- **State the rule once and cite it everywhere.** Each document reads well alone, which is exactly
  the pull toward restating — and this repository has recorded twice that a restated rule drifts.
  Citing also made the change checkable: the old string's *absence* on six surfaces is a test row.
- **Enumerate the seams and make the enumeration the deliverable.** The ticket said it would be
  judged on the list being complete rather than on the first two entries, and the seam that mattered
  — replan — was the third.
- **Measure before designing around a cost.** The advisory's first implementation composed the
  existing per-mission counter, was correct, and took over ten minutes; the measurement is what
  turned it into one pass.
