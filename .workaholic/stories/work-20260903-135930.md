## 1. Overview

A `verification_handoff` was written once and never measured again, so a blocker true the day it
was written stayed true forever and the work behind it stopped being attempted. It is now a
**probe** — a command whose exit status decides — re-run every time the unit is claimed.

**Highlights:**

1. **`verification_handoff: probe: <command>`**, read by the one reader (which never runs it) and
   executed by a new claim-time runner with four outcomes.
2. **`clean` is not a handoff** — the unit takes its ordinary route — and `blocking` carries the
   probe's **own output** as the reason instead of a sentence.
3. **Prose stays valid and is classed `unmeasured`**: not false, not true, nobody can re-probe it.

## 2. Motivation

The ask's measurement was taken elsewhere — four pull requests parked, **three** of the declarations
false when finally probed: a key already on disk, a secret-put CI that had already run, an entrance
whose tokens were held. The first ticket required taking the same reading **here** before fixing a
format, and it changed the design: among the **32 queued tickets, zero** declare anything, and all
**six** declarations anywhere in the tree are prose sentences, **none** a command. A format that
replaced prose would have invalidated every declaration that exists.

There is also a circularity worth naming: an `awaiting_verification` claim is **excluded from the
offer**, so "the next claim will re-check it" is false — the next claim never comes. That is why the
re-probe had to reach `/moderate` as well as the route.

## 3. What changed

The one reader gained `probe` / `measurable` / `unmeasured` and **runs nothing**;
`run-verification-probe.sh` is the runner, bounded by `WORKAHOLIC_PROBE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` (60) and
`WORKAHOLIC_PROBE_OUTPUT_MAX` (2000); `workaholic:drive` §6 routes on its word;
`/moderate`'s `handoff-units` step re-probes rather than re-reading the sentence; and the writing
seam says to prefer a probe and what makes a good one.

## 4. Concerns

**A probe runs on every claim, so it must be safe to.** The writing seam says so explicitly —
read-only, bounded, no side effect — but nothing mechanical can enforce it: the command is the
operator's, and running it is the whole point. The bound is a stated writing rule, not a check.

**An absence of a reading is never `clean`.** A timeout, a missing unit or a failed reader all
leave the declaration **standing**. The direction of failure is chosen deliberately, the same way
the base-checks reader chooses it: a wrong `clean` merges work nobody could verify.

**`/moderate` clears no handoff.** On a `clean` probe it says the declaration has gone false and
asks; it does not unpark the unit, because that step asks and does nothing else. The repair lands on
the acting path instead — the next `/implement` claim re-derives the same `clean` and proceeds.

**Nothing mechanical can see a session inheriting a belief.** The contagion bound is prose in the
skill; what is load-bearing is that the reader is called per unit and answers only from that unit's
own members, so a run that follows the route cannot inherit anything.

## 5. Successful development patterns

**Measure before fixing a format, even when the ask already measured.** The ask's numbers came from
a consuming repository; this repository's own reading — zero declarations queued, six prose ones in
history — is what made the format additive rather than a replacement. A replacement would have
parked the work those six name with no way to unpark it.

**Prove that a reader is a reader.** The probe used in the suite is `touch <canary>`, and the
assertion is that the canary does **not** exist afterwards. That is a much stronger statement than
reading the code and believing it.

**Give the absence its own word early.** `unmeasured` was cheap to add while the classes were being
invented and would have been expensive to retrofit once `clean`/`blocking` were load-bearing —
the third value is what lets every existing declaration keep working untouched.
