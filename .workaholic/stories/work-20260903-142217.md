## 1. Overview

The loop runs forever, so its **fixed per-tick cost** is the number that matters, and it was larger
than the work most ticks do — measured over two hours as ~23 ticks, 4 capturing an ask, the rest
quiet.

**Highlights:**

1. **The cadence gate reads one line.** `log-read.sh --latest-tick` answers the newest tick and
   carries **no entries** — 50,087 bytes of day log replaced by a timestamp.
2. **The command's record left its body**, verbatim, into `skills/loops/reference/tick-record.md`.
3. **A quiet tick says one line**, a run's result reaches the parent **once**, and the channel is
   read in the concise format.

## 2. Motivation

Every item is a measurement, and the largest lever is the one paid whether or not anything happened:
the command body is 11,291 bytes re-paid every five minutes in a session that never resets. Beside
it: a day log read twelve times an hour to answer one timestamp and growing until the day rolls
over; a thirty-one step table delivered twice into the parent's context; four to six lines of
`still_running` / `not_due` on ticks with nothing to say; and a channel read asking for
reactions and thread metadata nothing consumes.

## 3. What changed

`--latest-tick` on the one log reader (every filter still applies, so it also answers *when did a
step last run*); the concise format named with its reason; the per-loop line gated on something
having happened; the deliver-once rule stated in the spawn section; and five record passages moved
to a reference page, each replaced by a citation.

## 4. Concerns

**The body is larger than it started and the report says so.** It was 18,373 bytes; the other four
tickets **added** ~1.4 KB of operative text and the split removed ~2.4 KB of record, leaving 19,090.
Quoting the 2.4 KB alone would be the more flattering number and the less true one. What the split
bought is a place for the next one to put what it finds.

**`--latest-tick` builds the rows it then discards.** The saving is entirely in what crosses the
boundary to the caller, which is where the 50 KB was being paid; a caller-side saving is the whole
of the ticket's ask, and a reader-side one would be a different change.

**"Nothing operative moved" is unfalsifiable as prose.** The suite row therefore **names** the
instructions that must remain — the Slack window, the dedup ledger, the capture, the propose
cadence, the moderate gate, the checkout read, and the two post shapes — and asserts each is still
in the command. A later split that takes one out goes red on that instruction by name.

## 5. Successful development patterns

**Name what a rule must keep, not what it must not lose.** The split's hard bound was *no operative
instruction moves*, which no check can express. Turning it into a list of instructions that must
still be present made it checkable, and the list is short because operative text is.

**An empty answer needs its reading stated at the source.** `latest_tick: ""` could be read as
*just now*, which would silence the gate it exists to feed. The header says it means *no such tick*,
and the suite asserts the empty string rather than accepting any falsy value.

**A cheap flag can pay for itself twice.** `--latest-tick` keeps every existing filter, so
`--step-prefix foo --latest-tick` answers *when did a `foo…` step last run* — a second use nobody
asked for, obtained by not special-casing the new mode.
