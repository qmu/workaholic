## 1. Overview

`list-catchable-claims.sh` offered `report_undelivered` and `queue_drained` only. An
`awaiting_verification` claim — a handoff pull request the route opens and deliberately leaves
open, waiting on a person — was excluded, so the one class of branch **guaranteed by design** to
sit open for a long time was the one class the catch-up never brought forward.

**Highlights:**

1. The candidate set admits `awaiting_verification`, in both places the reader names a verdict.
2. The delivery half is untouched and stays `queue_drained`-only, with its second reason written
   down: a person is the next actor by construction.
3. `catch-up-claim.sh` gains no verdict gate at all — it never had one — and now names why it did
   not deliver: `delivery: not_attempted: awaiting_verification`.

## 2. Motivation

The handoff route's contract says the pull request opens and **stays open**. It does not say the
work on it should decay while it waits, and nothing in the loop was keeping it current.

Measured on this repository at 2026-09-07T08:20+09:00, before the change:

- Claim `work-20260906-023953`, unit `report-each-tick-in-the-originating-codex-chat`, PR **#993**,
  opened 2026-09-06T04:13+09:00 — about **25 hours** standing.
- `list-claims.sh`: `resume_reason: awaiting_verification`, `mergeability: content`,
  `mergeability_content_files` naming three hand-written paths, author this identity.
- `list-catchable-claims.sh`: `count: 0`, `candidates: []`.

Six of that unit's seven tickets are already driven and archived on the branch. The seventh holds
a genuine prose `verification_handoff:` for a live run in the operator's own Codex chat. So the
branch is finished work waiting on one human act, and every merge into `main` since made it
harder to land — while the reader whose whole job is *which of my branches can I still bring
forward* offered nothing at all.

## 3. Journey

**The diagnosis came before the fix, and it changed what needed changing.** The ticket recorded
the reporter's proposed shape as a hypothesis with three terms to test:

1. **Reproduce.** The live state above, measured rather than taken from the ticket's word.
2. **Does the act need widening?** No. `catch-up-claim.sh` consults `VERDICT` exactly three
   times — `foreign_identity`/`identity_unresolved`, `claim_active`, and the delivery bound — and
   has **no entry-side verdict gate**. An `awaiting_verification` unit handed to it already
   catches up, pushes, and falls through to `report caught_up ""`. So the whole change is the
   candidate set plus one named reason.
3. **Does the reader's own stated reasoning survive?** Yes. Its header refuses to add a REST call
   per candidate on the ground that both admitted verdicts already *mean* "at an open pull
   request". In `lib/claims.sh`, `awaiting_verification` is assigned inside the
   `elif [ "$_cs_reported" = "true" ]` arm, so it carries the same term and costs no lookup.

**What the widening reaches beyond its own script.** `claimable-units.sh` composes this reader,
so the `catchable` term of the tick's claimable count rises for such a unit. That is the intended
effect under *claimable means work an `/implement` pass would act on* — a handoff branch needing a
catch-up **is** such work — and it put three documents out of date, each of which said
`awaiting_verification` "stays uncounted". They now say it stays uncounted **as a unit to claim**
and is reached through the `catchable` term, exactly as `parked_with_pr` already was.

**The registration was the part with teeth.** `scripts/test-workflow-scripts.mjs` pins the
verdict's consumers in both directions: every file that reads `awaiting_verification` outside a
comment must be one the enumeration accounts for. Widening the reader turned that row red until
`list-catchable-claims.sh` and `catch-up-claim.sh` were registered — in the suite **and** in the
`awaiting_verification` row of `drive/reference/claims.md`, as the verdict's one enumerated
**acting** consumer under *When a bounded act may read a judgement*, whose four clauses it meets:
it re-derives the verdict off the live row at the moment of the act, is idempotent
(`already_current` touches no ref), is reversible (a merge commit, never a rewrite), and refuses
every bound by its own word.

**The delivery bound was written to stay one line.** Setting the named reason *inside* the bound
would have replaced the literal the suite pins; it is set on the line above instead, so the bound
below is byte-identical to what it was.

## 5. Concerns

- **The delivery bound is the load-bearing half and the widening makes it easy to lose.** It is
  now written down with *both* its reasons in four places, and a suite row fails on a change that
  merges a handoff pull request. A reader who finds only the `report_undelivered` reason will
  conclude the bound is about avoiding a double attempt.
- **`pull_request_reviewed` is expected to fire more often for this class.** A handoff pull request
  is open precisely so a person can look at it, so it is the class likeliest to carry a submitted
  review. The bound already exists, is three-valued, and was deliberately left exactly as it is.
- **A `content` class is a prediction, not a verdict.** The reader computes with the repository's
  `.gitattributes` out of reach and is pessimistic by construction; a hunk the merge itself cannot
  settle still refuses `content_conflict` with nothing pushed.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- **The diagnosis changed the design.** Steps 1-3 of the ticket established that the *act* needed
  no widening at all, which turned a two-script change into a candidate-set change plus one named
  reason — and made the suite's existing pin on the delivery bound survivable.
- **A guard that fails on an unregistered consumer is worth more than the prose it protects.**
  The enumeration row went red the moment the reader read a verdict it was not registered for,
  which is exactly the failure the table exists to catch.
