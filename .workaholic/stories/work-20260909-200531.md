## 1. Overview

A native `/work` tick reported implementation complete over zero merges, six queued tickets and two
unreconciled pull requests, after two runners stopped on one refused REST merge an operator then
completed by hand. Each of those three readings is now answerable from the tree.

**Highlights:**

1. A refused delivery names which capability refused it, and the coordinator gained the retry step.
2. A completion claim is reconciled against merges, claims and the queue before it is made.
3. A tightened constraint over persisted data must name its legacy fixture.

## 2. Motivation

Each failure was one reading standing in for another: a 403 on one call became a statement about
the session's ability to deliver, a worker's own `outcome` became evidence that work had landed,
and a migration passing against an empty database became evidence about production's rows. The
honest answer was available every time — the claim oracle, the branch story, the tree — and nothing
asked for it. Two of the three causes turned out to be findings in this tree rather than inferences
from the retrospective, which is why each ticket's step 1 was *localize before designing*.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. Name which capability refused a delivery ([de8db115f](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/de8db115f))

`branching/scripts/refusal-capability.sh` classifies a refusal as `no_capability` / `call_errored` /
`not_permitted` / `none`, beside `merge-reason.sh` which keeps owning the word. `merge-pull.sh` reads
it instead of spelling a literal verdict, and `commands/infinite-development.md` gained the
connector-retry step only `commands/implement.md` had.

### 3-2. Reconcile a completion claim before making it ([e1453acdf](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/e1453acdf))

`loops/scripts/reconcile-completion.sh` composes `act-effect.sh`, `list-claims.sh` and
`plan-units.sh` to answer merged units, standing claims (and whose they are) and the remaining
queue. `act-effect.sh` gained a `--claims` hand-back so reconciling N units costs one oracle read
rather than N+1.

### 3-3. Require legacy-row evidence for a tightened check ([b8124b17e](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/b8124b17e))

`rules/general.md` states that a constraint tightened over persisted data is verified against a
representative legacy fixture; the ticket format asks for that fixture and the upgrade run, and
`ship` states that a failed or pending deployment stays its own visible state.

## 4. Outcome

The three readings a tick reports are derived rather than relayed, and each degradation is named by
its own reason instead of collapsing into a confident zero. No gate was added, no refusal word
renamed and no token moved.

## 5. Concerns

### The legacy-row rule is absent from the canonical policy pages

- **Severity:** moderate
- **Description:** Ticket 3 asked for it in `persistence.md` / `test.md` / `ci-cd.md`, hard copies
  sourced from qmu.co.jp whose editing is out of scope by name — a local edit would be reverted at
  the next sync ([b8124b17e](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/b8124b17e), `rules/general.md`).
- **How to Fix:** Author the practice on qmu.co.jp; the ordinary sync PR brings the copies into line.

### The reconciliation is composed by nothing yet

- **Severity:** moderate
- **Description:** Both readers are wired in as prose — the only shape available, since no script
  may call an MCP tool — so whether a live tick runs them is checkable by nothing
  ([e1453acdf](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/e1453acdf), `commands/infinite-development.md`).
- **How to Fix:** Read the next tick reports for the counts and the `(<capability> on <route>)`
  suffix; a report naming neither is non-conformant and is the signal to look.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- *Localize before designing* found two defects and falsified half the retrospective's own diagnosis.
- A pinned **absence** is worth as much as a pinned presence where a sync would revert the filling.
- Composing a reader per unit exposed an N+1 cost that a hand-back flag removed, touching no word.
