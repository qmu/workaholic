## 1. Overview

`land-unit.sh` held the last inline copy of the release-scan tier reading, and its `case` over the scan's raw text had no default arm — so a scan that said nothing, or said `block` one byte differently, reported `scan_verdict: "pass"` and pushed the unit onto `main`. The reading now composes `gate-decision.sh`, three guards refuse rather than skip, and the refusal arm sits above the override.

**Highlights:**

1. Two of four measured scan shapes **landed** before this change; all four are now refused or read correctly.
2. `--override-scan` cannot rule past an unread scan — enforced by arm ordering, not a second condition.
3. `scan_verdict` gains no third value; a scan that ran behaves byte-identically.

## 2. Motivation

PR #1206 repaired `gate-decision.sh` so an unread scan refuses instead of passing, but that repair could not reach `land-unit.sh`, which never called the gate. It carried its own `case` over the scan's raw JSON, pinned to two different spellings of one producer's output, counting findings with `grep -o '"category":'`, and with no `*)` arm. Measured at `fc5b42b94` by running the block verbatim with the scan stubbed: a scan exiting 0 with no output landed the unit; a `block` carrying a `hard` secret finding spelled without the space landed it too; a scan exiting non-zero killed the script under `set -e` with no JSON at all, after the base had already been merged into the worktree.

That last push is the one in this plugin whose destination *is* the base ref, so *read nothing* and *read and found nothing* were byte-identical outputs at the seam where the difference matters most.

## 3. Changes

### 3-1. Refuse an unread scan in land-unit.sh ([3323f288c](https://github.com/qmu/workaholic/commit/3323f288c))

Replaced the inline tier reading with a composition of `release-scan/scripts/gate-decision.sh`, added three refusing guards (both scripts present, the scan's exit status read explicitly, an empty gate reading refused), ordered the `decision: "refuse"` arm above every block arm and above the override, and closed the `case` with a mandatory `*)`. `scan_findings` now comes from the gate's structural `total`.

## 4. Outcome

`land-unit.sh` is no longer a second home for the tier policy: `gate-decision.sh` is the one derivation and `publish-tree-pr.sh`, which reads the scan raw but ends its `case` in a `*)`, is the only remaining raw reader. A scan that ran is unchanged — a `hard` finding still refuses `secret_finding` non-overridably, an override-tier block still refuses `scan_block` without the flag and lands `overridden` with it.

## 5. Concerns

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- Reproducing all four shapes *before* touching the code made the fix's direction unarguable and caught that the unspaced block is the `secret_finding` it always was, not a `scan_unreadable` — a prediction the ticket got one step wrong.
- Asserting on the origin's `refs/heads/main` being byte-identical, rather than on the reported word, is what makes each new row a proof: two of the four shapes reported `{"landed": true, "scan_verdict": "pass"}` before the change **and pushed**.

## 7. Notes

The stated cost is that `land-unit.sh` acquires `jq` transitively through the gate. That is the correct direction — an absent or broken `jq` becomes `jq_unavailable`, which this change turns into a refusal rather than the silent land it would be under a text read. The script itself embeds no `jq`; it reads the gate's `reason` and `total` off that script's own fixed six-field object, which matters on the refusal arm in particular, where `jq` is exactly what may be missing.
