## 1. Overview

Three readers answered something other than the truth when their input was malformed, each in the
direction that reads as *nothing to do*: the moderation planner aborted to empty stdout on the
second tick of an hour and a caller read it as *no steps selected*; the concern extractor took an
unvalidated positional and stamped an unsubstituted flag onto the base twice; and the tick's
receipt vocabulary had no word for a person it decided not to answer. Each now refuses, or names,
by its own word — and the base's own `validate` check, red since `f195a667f`, is green again.

**Highlights:**

1. The planner's cause is **named and measured**: a registry row whose `trigger` is not an object,
   reached only on the arm a cold tick never evaluates. The registry's **size is not a term** —
   measured at 36, 40, 60, 100, 200 and 400 steps.
2. `extract-deferred-concerns.sh` refuses `flag_in_positional` / `bad_pr_number` above every write:
   no record, no branch, no publish tree.
3. `ack_withheld` is its own word beside `ack_failed`, and a tick carrying one is an **unanswered
   person** rather than a clean tick.

## 2. Motivation

The three tickets were queued separately and share one shape. `plan-steps.sh` read
`.trigger.seconds` inside a short-circuited `or`, so a malformed registry row planned cleanly on
the first tick of an hour and aborted mid-array on the second — through a pipe the pipeline then
exits 0, which is indistinguishable from an empty plan, and the reading exists precisely to stop a
step running twice inside its hour. `extract-deferred-concerns.sh` took its pull-request number
positionally and validated nothing, so a shifted vector reached `main` as the permanent squash
subject `f5b91d91b` and as `origin_pr: --base` on an append-only record — and, invisibly, let the
explicit-destination contract its header spends thirteen lines on fall through to its default.
And a person's ask sat twenty-five minutes while four ticks reported clean, because `ack_failed`
covered both *the transport refused it* and *we chose not to post it*.

## 6. Successful Development Patterns

- The planner ticket recorded two signals and **refused to assert a cause**, and that refusal is
  what made the repair correct: one signal (a 1024-byte cut) was an artefact that does not
  reproduce, and the other (a shape error over intact JSON) was the whole cause. A ticket that had
  picked one would have sent the repair at a size bound that does not exist.
- Reproducing before repairing paid twice — the planner's piped `exit 0` and the extractor's
  `origin_pr: --base` were both measured on this tree before a line changed.
- Proving a new assertion **goes red when its own sentence is reverted** is cheap (mutate the text
  in memory, re-run the predicate) and is the difference between a pin and a comment.
- Pinning one wording across two files has to normalise whitespace, or it pins line width and
  invites someone to reflow a file to satisfy a test.
