# Settle a stranded publication a generator can repair

## Overview

PROPOSED. The act the mission exists for: given a stranded publication whose only conflict
is one a generator settles, bring it back onto the base and deliver it, without a person.
The measured repair was three commands — merge the base, run `refresh-index.sh`, commit —
and every one of them already ships in this plugin. This ticket wires them behind one
bounded act; it invents no merge engine and no second classification rule.

## Policies

- `workaholic:implementation` / `policies/directory-structure.md` — conventional project layout
- `workaholic:implementation` / `policies/coding-standards.md` — style and structure conventions

## Key Files

- `plugins/workaholic/skills/drive/scripts/catch-up-claim.sh` — the act to model on: it
  composes `catchup-main.sh`, regenerates with the repository's own tooling, runs the fast
  checks, and refuses every bound by its own word.
- `plugins/workaholic/skills/ship/scripts/catchup-main.sh` — the one merge engine;
  `--resolve-mechanical` binds the caller to regenerate.
- `plugins/workaholic/skills/okf/scripts/refresh-index.sh` — the generator whose output the
  resolution re-derives.
- `plugins/workaholic/skills/drive/scripts/retry-undelivered.sh` — the delivery half to model
  on: one merge attempt through the same REST seam, no branch, no worktree, no claim.

## Implementation Steps

1. Add one act over the previous ticket's reader, taking a single publication. Re-derive its
   mergeability **at the moment of the act**, from the same reader, and refuse on anything
   but `mechanical`.
2. Resolve through `catchup-main.sh --resolve-mechanical` and regenerate with the
   repository's own tooling — never by editing the generated bytes, and never a second merge
   engine.
3. Run the repository's fast checks before pushing, exactly as `catch-up-claim.sh` does. A
   push that turns a check red costs more than the strand did.
4. Deliver: one merge attempt through the existing REST seam, in the existing merge
   vocabulary, and record the outcome in that vocabulary's own words.
5. Make it idempotent and bounded: `already_current` touches no ref; a second run over a
   delivered publication is a no-op; every refusal leaves the branch byte-identical, writes
   nothing, and exits 0 with its own word — at least `content_conflict`,
   `not_a_work_branch`, `has_claim_commit`, `scan_held:<tier>`, `validation_failed:<check>`,
   `push_failed`, and the operator-facing words the reader's boundary names.
6. State in the header that the act reads a **re-derived** verdict, is idempotent, is
   reversible, and refuses every bound by its own word — the four terms
   `drive/reference/claims.md` already requires of a bounded act.

## Quality Gate

**Acceptance criteria** — the checkable conditions that must hold:

- A publication colliding only on a generated region is caught up, validated, pushed and
  merged, with no worktree left behind.
- A publication colliding on content is refused `content_conflict` with the branch
  byte-identical and nothing pushed.
- A second run over either is a no-op reporting its own word.

**Verification method** — the commands/tests/probes that prove them:

- Hermetic rows in `scripts/test-workflow-scripts.mjs` for the mechanical and the content
  case, and for the re-run.
- `node scripts/test-workflow-scripts.mjs`

**Gate** — what must pass before approval:

- No second merge engine and no second classification rule in the diff.
- The act never force-pushes, never deletes a branch, and never closes a pull request.

## Considerations

- A scan finding is why some publications are left open deliberately. Refuse those by their
  own word rather than merging past them: no gate is ever overridden.
- The stated cost of catching a publication up is that its pull request gains a merge commit
  from the base. That is what `catch-up-claim.sh` already does for a claim, so it is
  consistent rather than new — say so in the header rather than leaving a reader to notice.
