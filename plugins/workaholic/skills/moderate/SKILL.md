# Moderate

The loop's **maintenance tick**. `[Specificate]` turns asks into work and `[Implement]` drives it; nothing keeps the space *around* them tidy — stale issues, GitHub↔`.workaholic/` drift, pull requests stuck after a failed auto-merge, documentation that no longer matches the concept. `/moderate` finds those, files them **through the existing seams**, and says what needs a human (issue #471).

Relocated detail: [the thirty-one-step contract](reference/workflow.md) — each step's inputs, what it may write, its abort reasons, and the ruling it is held to.

## The tick has a voice, and it is one thread an hour

**Root, then its questions inside it** (2026-08-21, the developer's design). Every step but the last reads and logs; the last one speaks. What it posts is **one root per tick** carrying what changed in the hour, with every question of that tick as a **mentioned reply inside that root's thread**:

- the **root** is orientation — addressed to nobody, no mention token, keyed `` `tick:<tick-id>` ``;
- the **replies** are directed — one named person each, keyed `` `ask:<key>` ``, asked once and never re-asked.

Two speech acts, one place to look, told apart by **position in the thread** rather than by two routines. Exact shapes: `workaholic:notify`, *The moderator's hourly thread*.

**What it can ask about is what its steps read**, which is why the asking steps were added one at a time and each is named where it lives: `stalled-units` (a claim that has not moved — never one whose work already reached the base, which is a fact for the log rather than a question, 2026-08-26), `direction-health` (a direction out of date, unanswered, since 2026-08-27 **arrived** — its work all in and nothing waiting, keyed `direction-arrived:<slug>` — or, since 2026-08-29, **expiring**: its date approaching, keyed `direction-expiring:<slug>`, so a direction is warned before its own date silences the loop rather than after) and, since 2026-08-26, **`unanswered-asks`** — a message on the repository's channel that nobody has answered, **mention or no mention**. Before it, the one surface in this plugin that names a person could not learn that a person was waiting: a question written in the channel reached somebody only if one of the tick's own readers happened to produce a row about it (`reference/workflow.md` §16).

**And, since 2026-08-27, `base-health`** — the base's own checks, asked of the attributed merge's author and keyed `base-red:<commit>` so one broken commit costs exactly one question however many ticks see it (`reference/workflow.md` §20). The loop merges its own work onto `main` every half hour and nothing read a check run, so a green base and a base nobody looked at were one reading; no other step could see it either, because `stuck-prs` and `merge-conflicts` read **pull requests** and find nothing wrong with one that already merged. It **asks and nothing else** — never a re-run, never a revert, never a merge — because what it reads is a **judgement**, not a proof: a re-run can turn a red check green (`workaholic:drive`, *Claims*; `drive/reference/claims.md`, *Proofs and judgements*). Nothing here gates on it, and the QA window still owns quality.

**And, since 2026-08-27, `handoff-units`** — a finished unit waiting on a verification only a person can run, asked of the **claim holder** and keyed `handoff-unit:<unit>` so it is asked exactly once (`reference/workflow.md` §21). It consumes the claim oracle's `awaiting_verification`: a reported unit whose still-**queued** work was *declared* unverifiable in an unattended environment at creation, which `workaholic:drive` §6 routed to the handoff route on purpose. Until it existed the verdict was read nowhere outside `drive/`, so nothing addressed anybody again — and once the tip went stale, `stalled-units` asked the wrong question about it; that step now filters the verdict and counts it instead, so **one step asks and the other filters** and a unit never draws two questions in two vocabularies. The **running identity is never consulted** and it reads `list-claims.sh`, never `plan-units.sh` (`undrivable-units`' two axes). The question names the declared reason **verbatim**, resolved per candidate by `drive/scripts/declared-handoff-detail.sh`, which composes `verification-handoff.sh` — still the one reader of the field. It **asks and nothing else**: it never clears a handoff, retries a verification, merges or closes the pull request, touches the claim, or withdraws the declaration, because `awaiting_verification` is a **judgement** (`drive/reference/claims.md`, *Proofs and judgements*). What did not move: the field's two writers are still `/ticket` and `/specificate`, a run never declares it for its own unit, and the verdict still does not forbid `ok`. The rival design — one unified *what the loop is blocked on* report across the four vocabularies — was refused: the four verdicts call for four different acts by four different people, and a single report addressed to nobody is precisely what `🔧 Needs a decision` and `📦 Release Preparation` were retired for.
**And, since 2026-08-30, `raced-units`** — a unit two runs are driving at the same time, asked of the **claim holders** and keyed `raced-unit:<unit>` so it is asked exactly once (`reference/workflow.md` §29). It consumes `claims_unit_resolution`'s **`ambiguous`**: two or more *live* claims for one unit, which every writer in the protocol refuses and which nothing asked anybody about — measured 2026-08-30, `work-20260830-055314` and `work-20260830-055318` were claimed for one unit four seconds apart and each drove the same four tickets for over an hour, while the run that lost reported an ordinary undelivered unit. No sibling could see the shape: `stalled-units` finds one claim that has not moved, `undelivered-units` one refused merge, `catchup-blocked` one conflicted branch, and each of those is a **consequence** whose question hides the cause, because both rows are individually healthy and what is wrong is that both exist. **This step asks and three siblings filter and count** — `stalled-units`, `undelivered-units` and `catchup-blocked`, through one shared `lib/raced-units.sh` over the scan each has already made, so no second walk of the refs and no second definition of a race exists; `retire-claims` needs no change, because its candidates are `superseded` rows and an `ambiguous` unit has none by definition. **The aftermath is deliberately not a candidate**: one live claim beside a `superseded` one is byte-identical to the sanctioned recovery in which a superseded claim's work is taken on a fresh claim, and telling them apart would need a clock threshold or a stored field, both refused by name here. The question **names both branches and picks between neither**, which is `ambiguous_claim`'s standing everywhere in the protocol. It **asks and nothing else**: no claim released, no branch deleted or chosen, no pull request merged or closed, no gate lifted — every reading it carries is a **judgement** (`drive/reference/claims.md`, *Whether a unit is being driven twice*). What it cannot do is stop the race happening: that needs an arbitration this container's transport refuses, recorded as its mission's own finding.

**And, since 2026-08-31, `blocked-tick`** — a tick that **opened and never closed**, keyed `blocked-tick:<tick-id>` so a stopped hour costs one question however many later ticks see it (`reference/workflow.md` §31). It is the one reading here whose subject is *this tick's own machinery*: three consecutive ticks sat at `requires_action` waiting on a permission prompt a routine has nobody to answer, and the base carried no trace of any of them, because `persist-log.sh` was the closing act and the record that would show a stop is the record the stop prevents. `run.sh`'s **opening persist** put the opening there and this reads for it — neither half is worth anything alone. It reads **the tick before last**, so a merely slow run is not reported, and *closed* means a `human-checkin` line rather than the persist's own, which never reaches the base on the tick that wrote it. Addressed to **nobody** (a stopped tick is a fact about the repository), and it **says what is known and no more**: the reason is not recoverable from the base by construction, so the question never guesses one.

**And, since 2026-08-31, `cadence-lapse`** — a declared periodic artifact whose newest one is older than its period allows, keyed `cadence-lapsed:<name>` so one lapse costs one question however many ticks see it (`reference/workflow.md` §30). Every other step here is driven by an object that **exists** — an open pull request, a commit, a claim, a ticket, a record — so a producer that dies produces nothing and no step has anything to find: measured, a daily record stopped for four days while hourly ticks ran throughout and none of them reported it. This tick watches presence; this is the one step that watches **absence**. What it may notice is only what the repository declared (*Where a cadence is declared, and what it says*), so a repository declaring nothing is `skipped` by name and byte-identical to one before the step existed. It is **addressed to nobody** — the declaration names a cadence, a pattern and a period and no person, and the step will not stamp an identity nothing verified — and the running identity is never consulted, because a lapsed cadence is a fact about the repository. It **asks and nothing else**: it re-runs no routine, writes no artifact to satisfy a cadence, repairs no declaration and touches no claim; and it says plainly that it can see the artifact stopped and cannot see **why**, which is exactly why the finding is classified `needs_ruling`.


**A change is derived, never declared.** `render-tick-post.sh` calls a step changed when its summary differs from **the same step's summary in the previous tick** — read out of the log this tick already keeps. No step gained a field, no cursor is stored, and an unchanged answer cannot be restated, which is the property that lets an hourly root exist at all (`📦 Release Preparation` was retired for exactly that failure).

**One gate, and a question is it** (2026-08-22, issue #569 — the ticket's Open Decision, ruled there). The tick posts when it has **at least one question** — plus two narrow exceptions added since, each on its own stated grounds and neither touching that expression: the **morning digest** (2026-08-24) and a **check-in that reached nobody** (2026-08-28, below). `idle` (nothing changed, nothing to ask), **`no_question`** (changes, but nothing to ask), `no_previous_tick` and `no_log` all post nothing — and `no_log` is reported separately, because a mechanism that could not read must never announce quiet.

It was two gates, OR'd — a question **or** a changed step — and the second half is retired. The root's stated reason to exist is that it *carries* the tick's questions beneath it, told apart from them by position in the thread; with `0 question(s)` it is a status line addressed to nobody, which is exactly what `🔧 Needs a decision` and `📦 Release Preparation` were retired for. Measured four consecutive hours on a consuming repository: every post read `2 change(s), 0 question(s)` with nothing behind it.

**The alternative was weighed and rejected**: let a *named class* of change (a merge conflict appearing, an auto-merge failing, a target starting to need a human) earn a question-less root. It is defensible, but it keeps a line nobody is asked to act on, needs a list maintained per step, and the developer — shown the post twice — said it was of no use to anybody. A change worth a person's attention can be asked about; one that cannot be is a log entry, and the tick log already keeps every one of them. **The cost, stated rather than hidden**: a real change with no question attached is visible only in `.workaholic/moderations/`.

**And the diff is taken over a stable form, not the raw summary** (same change). A change is a step whose summary differs from the same step's an hour ago, and it was a raw string compare — while `inbound-sweep` embeds an ISO8601 timestamp in its summary and `doc-drift` embeds a sha. Both moved every tick *by construction*, so both were always "changed" and the derivation's own claim — that a diff cannot restate an unchanged answer — did not hold. `render-tick-post.sh` now normalizes a timestamp, a bare hex object name and a clock time out of both sides before comparing, and only those: stripping more would hide a real change behind noise, which is the opposite defect.

**It works business days and business hours.** `ask-question.sh` holds the per-tick cap (5), the daily bound (10), the quiet-hours window, and — since 2026-08-21 — a **working-day gate** (`WORKAHOLIC_WORK_DAYS`, default `1-5`). The weekend gate is not cosmetic: a question found at 10:00 on a Sunday used to be posted where nobody was reading, and the asked-once gate then guaranteed it was never posted again on a day somebody was. **Held is not dropped** — it waits for Monday.

**The daily bound counts *today*, and a spent day holds rather than drops** (2026-08-28, mission `deliver-what-the-loop-already-knows-to-the-person-who-can-act`). Four properties, stated here because the gap they were missing from is where the defect lived for eleven days:

- it counts the `human-checkin-ask` lines on the current **`WORKAHOLIC_QUIET_TZ` day** — the same day derivation the `quiet_hours` and working-day gates use, so the loop has **one notion of a day**;
- a spent cap **holds** the question, never drops it;
- a held question is **re-offered on the next eligible tick, oldest-held first** (`step-human-checkin.sh`, below);
- the bound is passed to `log-read.sh`'s existing `--since`, so there is no second reader and no stored cursor.

It counted **every day the log had ever held**. `asked_today` was the unbounded prefix count, the log is append-only and never pruned by a machine, so the count only ever grew: once the all-time total crossed `max_per_day` every question was refused `day_cap` forever. Measured on the live tree — `count: 12, days: 5` against a cap of 10, a fresh key on a working weekday at 14:00 refused with `asked_today: 12`, the same reader bounded to the current Asia/Tokyo day answering `count: 0`; eight consecutive ticks reported `ok` and posted nothing while a red base, a 31-hour declared handoff, three undeletable branches and seven undrivable units sat held behind it. **The repair was a bound passed to a reader that already accepted one**, and deliberately not: a raised cap, a second reader, a stored cursor, or a second notion of a day. The day boundary moves in the quiet-hours zone while the log's **files** are keyed by UTC day, so near the boundary the bound can include a UTC file whose later entries belong to the local next day — it **over-counts rather than under-counts**, holding a question rather than asking a duplicate, which is the safe direction and is stated so a later reader does not "fix" it the other way.

**The arrears drain in the order they went stale.** `step-human-checkin.sh` hands `held` back ordered by the day each key was **first** held (a key held across several ticks is as old as its first hold), tie-broken on the tick id and then the key so the order is total and a re-entered tick produces a byte-identical sequence. The day is already in the log the tick keeps, so this adds no ledger, no cursor and no field. **The step orders; it neither caps nor asks** — `max_per_tick` stays with `ask-question.sh`, `held_count` counts the whole set rather than a prefix, and the order is a *proposal*: the Recommended-label test still applies per candidate. Age is used rather than urgency deliberately — a severity ranking across the step vocabularies is a judgement no script can make, and the verdicts call for different acts by different people, which is why one unified blocked-on report was refused.

**And a tick that reached nobody says so.** The step reads back `delivered`, `held` and `candidates`, and names why a tick delivered nothing: **`cap_spent`** (the day's budget was spent — the mechanism worked), **`cap_unbounded`** (the day count could not be bounded — **our own degradation**, never rendered as `cap_spent`, which is the whole point of the split), `all_held` (the quiet window, the off day or the per-tick cap), `all_asked_before`, `no_candidates`. An unreadable log is `status: degraded` with the reader's own reason and **no `delivered` claim**; an *absent* log area is a readable answer. Whether the tick could deliver at all is **asked of the gate** — one probe with a key unique to the tick, recorded nowhere — so the day's arithmetic keeps one home and the step cannot disagree with the gate the agent is about to run. What `delivered` honestly is: the agent asks *after* `run.sh` returns and there is no post-agent seam in the run to move the reading to, so on the step's own pass it is zero by construction and the reason words are the ones the step can **observe** — the caps and the holds — never a prediction of the agent's judgement.

**That failure is the third gate on the root**, beside the question gate and the morning digest and on the digest's own precedent — the question gate's own expression is untouched. It fires only when the check-in supplied an event, which it does only on `cap_spent` or `cap_unbounded`, and only when that reading **changed** since the previous tick, so it is said once and stops entirely once the channel is delivering. It is **not** the retired changed-step half of the gate, which let any changed step earn a question-less root. Before it, a tick with 22 candidates and zero delivered posted **nothing at all**, and total silence was byte-identical to a quiet hour.

**What it costs, stated rather than hidden**: a person following one item's own thread no longer sees the tick's question there. Every root line links the item it is about.

**A question a person cannot answer without opening a skill is a question that waits**, so the step supplies the wording, because it is what knows what its finding means. **What that wording must carry is `workaholic:notify`'s composition contract** (beside the `🙋 <@U…>` shape, 2026-08-31) — lead with what happened in words a reader outside the repository understands, name the identifier after it, never let a verdict word or a key stand alone, and give the body the one act asked of the addressee. It is stated **there and nowhere else**: restating it here is how two copies start disagreeing about the voice. The shape it is poured into (`🙋 <@U…>`, the mention token, one sentence beneath) is that catalog's too and is not a step's to move. **Every question-asking step's own spec was rewritten to that contract in one change** (2026-08-31) — thirteen steps and twenty keys, in `reference/workflow.md`, each naming what its heading leads with and the one act its body asks for, so the operator meets one voice rather than thirteen. **No key, cap, hold, addressee or age reading moved with it**, and no script was touched: `already_asked` keys on the step id `lib/question-id.sh` derives from the key, never on the text, which is what makes a wording sweep safe.

**And the mention only fires because a bot posts it** (2026-08-31, mission `notify-the-person-a-directed-question-addresses`). Every post reaches Slack as the operator's own account, and Slack notifies nobody of their own message — so in the single-developer configuration the one token this catalog keeps unconditionally resolved to the poster and paged nobody. The **root** stays the connector's; the **question reply** is posted by the tokened transport into the root's own `thread_ts`, which the connector returned when it posted the root, so no query is added and the reply stays inside the same thread. With no bot token it goes out through the connector exactly as today, and the step reports the surface that carried each question (`bot` / `connector` / the transport's refusal word) so one that reached nobody is never recorded as one that did. `ask-question.sh` is **byte-identical**: the key, the caps, the holds, `already_asked`, `answered` and the one bounded re-ask do not move, and neither does the wording — only the account that speaks it. The carrier rule itself lives in `workaholic:notify`, *Which transport carries which shape, and why* (`reference/workflow.md` §13).

- **The act is named in the operator's vocabulary, never in ours** — *announce that it ended*, not *call `close.sh`*. The announcement is the sanctioned route and the script is not theirs to run.
- **Every question says what the loop will not do**, so that *it still stands* reads as a complete answer that costs nothing further. No question offers a button, and none implies the tick will act on the answer itself.
- **It describes the state, never the person.** A direction filed an hour ago reads `dormant` correctly; "nothing has answered it yet" is a fact about the direction, and an accusation would be a fact about nobody.
- **A reading is described, never asserted as a verdict** (2026-08-27). The `direction-arrived:<slug>` question says everything attributed to the direction has landed and nothing is waiting, names **what landed and the date**, and asks — it never says the direction is *finished*. A strategy's "Reached when" is prose no script reads, so arrival is a **candidate**; the operator's answer decides, and the tick closes nothing either way. **And since 2026-08-28 it names what the reading could not see**: the unattributed active missions by slug with their queued-ticket counts, bounded to three names with the rest counted. *Everything attributed has landed* was true and **partial**, and the operator could not see which half they were ruling on. The residue is carried from the survey row, never re-read here; a **degraded** residue read yields no `arrived` reading and therefore no question at all (`workaholic:propose`, `workaholic:strategy`). **And since 2026-08-28 the `arrived` and `overdue` questions both name the whole leaving** (mission `make-a-direction-s-end-a-turn-of-the-loop-not-its-stop`): what the direction **never reached** beside what **no direction claimed** — asked *before* the decision, where it is evidence rather than a post-mortem. It is **carried, never composed here**: `direction-state.sh --with-leaving` attaches `closing-residue.sh`'s composition to the row and this step calls none of the three readers itself, at no extra read and no extra network call. The named detail rides the heading and only the size rides the body, which `workaholic:notify` reserves for the operator's act; a **degraded** leaving renders nothing, suppresses nothing, and is counted in the log-facing summary. **And a fifth reading since 2026-08-29** (mission `warn-a-direction-before-its-date-silences-the-loop`): a direction whose date is **approaching**, keyed `direction-expiring:<slug>` and addressed to its assignee. Every other reading answers backwards, so a live, in-date, `on_course` direction one day from its `target_date` drew no question at all and the day after `past_target_date` silenced origination with the only signal being `direction-overdue`, asked in **arrears** — `direction-last:<slug>`'s shape by a different cause. Its heading names **the days left and the date** (a warning that does not say how long somebody has is not a warning) with the leaving beside it, and its body names the same act `overdue` names, offered while it can still be taken. Every gate, cap and hold applies unchanged; it is **never held by an open ruling**, for `overdue`'s own reason — a ruling answers which direction a mission belongs to and answers nothing about a date — and the step re-dates, closes and amends nothing. **And `direction-last:<slug>` is the reading one step earlier**: `direction-none` fires only once every direction is already closed and is addressed to **nobody**, so the **last live** direction is named to the person who owns it while they can still act — derived from `active_count`, silent with more than one live direction and silent for a direction that already has a non-`live` question this tick, because one direction drawing two questions is the doubling `handoff-units` and `stalled-units` were split to avoid.
- **Nothing parses the answer.** `record-answer.sh` stores prose exactly as it always has; acting on it stays the next run's judgement.
- **And an answer written in the question's own thread reaches that writer** (2026-08-28, mission `let-an-answer-in-the-thread-turn-back-into-the-loop-s-work`). It reached nothing until then: a reply in the `🔎 Moderation` thread is not a channel message, so `unanswered-asks` cannot see it, the `:40` sweep excludes answers to the tick's own questions by rule, and `record-answer.sh` was reachable only from the moderator's own session — one session per answer. The `question-answers` step names the outstanding questions and **the coordinate each was posted at**, which `ask-question.sh --record-ask` wrote onto the ask line at post time, and hands one `slack_read_thread` per candidate back to the agent — **no search, no channel history**, and no new store, no second inbox and no second writer. What it does: the person's words go to `record-answer.sh`, an answer that **asks for something** becomes one `[FB]` issue through `file-inbound-ask.sh`, and an answer this run recorded carries the catalog's reaction on its own message. What it does not: **no reply is posted for this event**, a machine's post is never an answer, an unsure read records nothing and says why, and the filing and the stamp are never load-bearing on the recording. The dedup is structural — an `answered` question is not a candidate — so one answer is read, filed and stamped once however many ticks run (`reference/workflow.md` §22).
- **And what became of that answer is readable** (2026-08-31, mission `make-the-tick-s-questions-readable-and-close-them-in-the-thread`). `answer-outcome.sh --key <content-key>` answers `settled:nothing_filed` / `settled:issue_closed` / `pending` / `unreadable:<reason>` per answered question, composing `question-state.sh`, `log-read.sh` and the one GitHub transport — no new store, no cursor, no field on any artifact, and **one bounded REST read per *filed* candidate and none for the rest**. The chain from question to issue is the log's own `question-answers-filed-<slug>` line, never a search. A question with **no recorded answer** is refused by name (`not_answered:<state>`) rather than called `unreadable` — nothing has become of anything, which is not a reading we could not make. Every value is a **judgement** (`workaholic:drive`'s `reference/claims.md`, *Whether a recorded answer has been acted on*): an issue can be closed and reopened between two reads, so nothing may merge, close, gate, hold work or re-ask on it.

Like the `## Open Decisions` floor, this is a **prose contract, not a script gate** — no mechanical check tells a real three-part question from a padded one. What it buys is that a question missing one of the three is visibly non-conformant.

## Standing rules

- **Unattended by contract**, exactly as `/implement` and `/specificate` are: **no `AskUserQuestion` at any step**. Step 10 asks humans things, and it asks them *in Slack* — a routine-fired session has no question mechanism, so "ask a human" and "prompt the operator" are different acts here.
- **It files, it does not invent a home.** A finding becomes a **feedback record** (`workaholic:feedback`), work becomes a **ticket** or a **mission** through the seams that already publish them, and a question becomes a **Slack post** (`workaholic:notify`). The tick log records that it did; nothing else is ever written into `moderations/`. **The log and the root have two audiences, and each step writes for both** (2026-08-23): `summary` is the log-facing line — the audit trail a maintainer reads when the tick misbehaves, keeping every counter — and `event` is the post-facing one, naming what happened **to the repository**. A root line used to be the log summary rendered verbatim, so it carried `1 to judge`, `0 already captured` and `0 finding(s) already filed by an earlier tick`, and `no new documentation drift` announced that *nothing happened* as though it were a change. The step supplies the event because it knows what its finding means and the renderer does not; **a step with no event renders no line**, independently of the change diff, and the log's summaries are untouched. **A record it writes reaches the base on the log's own commit** (2026-08-23): `create.sh` stages a file and stops, and a routine's container is discarded, so a finding the sweep or the triage wrote was reported filed and then lost. `persist-log.sh --record <path>` carries the tick's own records — named one by one, never a sweep of whatever is staged — with no `work-*` branch, no claim, no pull request and no merge, exactly as the log travels; a record already on the base is left untouched, because a feedback record is immutable. **And a `<step>-filed` line proves nothing on its own**: `filed-records.sh` asks the tree whether the paths a line names are there, and an `unlanded` one is treated as **not filed** so the next tick re-derives the finding. Measured — the persist reported both records filed while the base carried the log and not one record, and the next tick skipped both findings on the strength of those lines.
- **A finding has three destinations, and only a `repairable` one becomes work** (2026-08-29, mission `let-the-tick-s-own-findings-become-the-loop-s-work`). It had two — a **question** to a person and a **feedback record** — and neither becomes work, because `[Specificate]`'s unattended entrance reads GitHub **issues**; so the loop's own debt piled up where nothing could drive it, while the only in-tick caller of the one filer acted on a *person's* answer. The third destination is an `[FB]` issue, filed by the `file-findings` step through `propose/scripts/file-inbound-ask.sh` — still the **one** filer — carrying the direction through `feedback/scripts/ask-feedback-line.sh`, still the one writer of that line. **Which findings may take it is a closed table with one home** (`reference/workflow.md`), keyed on the step id `run.sh`'s own `STEPS` already fixes and **read** rather than restated, with an unclassified step reading `needs_ruling`: mislabelling a ruling as mechanical is the failure the classification exists to prevent, so a new step is silent until somebody classifies it. The question is **who must act**, never how severe the finding is. The step **writes nothing** — not even its own log line — and its `event` is **always empty**, `standing-rulings`' rule for `standing-rulings`' reason: the agent files after `run.sh` returns, so an event here would announce an act the step has not taken.
- **A degraded read is reported and skipped, never half-applied.** An unreachable connector, an unreadable inbox and a 403 are each reported **by name** — never rendered as a step that ran and found nothing. That distinction is the one `list-inbound-issues.sh` already makes, and an hourly routine lives on it: a tick that reports "nothing to do" when it could not look is a tick that lies about its own coverage.

- **A refused action is reported, never silently skipped** (2026-08-31, mission `stop-an-unattended-tick-from-waiting-on-a-person`). `rules/interaction.md`, *An unattended run never waits for a person*, admits two outcomes and its second is *refuse the single action and carry on, recording what was refused and why* — admissible only if the record reaches somebody, because without one a refusal is indistinguishable from an action that silently did nothing, which is the shape this whole tick exists to remove. **A refusal record carries three facts and no more**: the action refused, the reason, and that the rest of the run continued. A refusal is not a stack trace. **It uses the status vocabulary that already exists**: the step reports **`blocked`** with its own reason and a summary naming those three facts, so the refusal rides the tick log line and the run report a person already reads, is never `ok`, and appears in the root's impairment clause beside a `degraded` read — which is why no new status, no new store and no field on any artifact was added. The agent's own refusals, taken after `run.sh` returns, are recorded the same way through `log-append.sh` under `<step>-refused`. **It moves no token and gates nothing**: a refused action is a fact about one step, not a verdict on the run. **What it records is a step's OWN declared refusal, and that limit is stated rather than glossed**: a permission prompt denied by the harness is not observable from inside a script at all (`workaholic:workaholify`, *Where an unattended run's prompt policy is configured*) and surfaces, if at all, as an ordinary `step_error` — so this contract covers the refusals this repository's own code decides to make, and claims no coverage of the ones it cannot see.
- **And it is reported where the OPERATOR reads, not only in the log** (2026-08-31, mission `name-the-steps-a-tick-could-not-read`). The rule above stopped at the tick log: `run.sh` classified every step `ok|filed|skipped|degraded|blocked` with a reason, wrote it down faithfully, and `render-tick-post.sh` read neither field — so the coverage the tick honestly recorded reached nobody. **Measured: 24 of 25 consecutive ticks with six steps impaired, discovered four days later because somebody asked.** A log nobody opens is not a report. Every root the tick posts now carries the count of impaired steps in its head and their names and reasons in its body (`workaholic:notify`; the derivation, the bound and the gate are `reference/workflow.md` §*The post this step produces*), and a **changed** impairment earns a root of its own even with no question — the fourth gate, beside the morning digest and the check-in that reached nobody. The **statement** is outside the change diff, because a step degraded the same way for a day has an unchanged summary and would otherwise be said once and vanish; the **post** is inside it, because an unchanged answer restated hourly is what `📦 Release Preparation` was retired for. `skipped` is deliberately not impairment: a step declining to run for a stated, healthy reason did not fail to see.
- **GitHub over REST only** — `gather/scripts/gh-rest.sh`. Never `gh issue …`, `gh pr …` or `gh repo …`: they are GraphQL-backed and a Claude Code Web session may 403 mid-run (`rules/shell.md`).
- **It never merges, never pushes into somebody else's branch, and never edits a live strategy.** The claim protocol owns `work-*` branches and the strategy layer has three writers — `create.sh`, `amend.sh`, `close.sh` — and this tick is none of them; it reports what it finds in both and lets their owners act. `direction-health` still only **asks**: revising a direction is announced by the operator and reaches `amend.sh` through `/specificate`, never from here (2026-08-27). That holds for the `arrived` reading too: a reading that a direction looks finished is one step from a routine that closes it, and the suite pins that neither `direction-health` nor `direction-state.sh` can reach any of the three writers, and that `close.sh` is reached from exactly one place in the plugin — `/specificate`'s *ended* route.

## The run

```bash
bash ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/moderate/scripts/run.sh
```

One invocation is one **tick**. It mints the tick id (`tick-id.sh`, UTC — every later write in the same tick is passed that id), runs the thirty-one steps **in order**, writes **one log line per step** into `.workaholic/moderations/<UTC-day>.md`, and returns the report as JSON. The step list lives in `run.sh`, not in this prose: every step is invoked and every step contributes a line, so a step that is missing, crashes, or prints nothing is reported `degraded` with its reason instead of vanishing from the report.

`--deadline-seconds <n>` bounds a tick. Steps not reached are logged `skipped` with reason `budget`, **by name** — a step that ran out of clock and a step that found nothing must never read the same.

Then, for every step that returned entries in `needs_agent`, act on them through the seam that step's section names and record what you actually did:

```bash
bash ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/moderate/scripts/log-append.sh \
  --tick <tick> --step <step>-filed --status filed --summary "<what was filed, with its id>"
```

`<step>-filed` is a **second, distinct fact** from the probe's own line — what the tick found versus what it filed — and the log is idempotent per `(tick, step)`, so the two never overwrite each other. Both survive a re-entered tick.

**Then persist again — this is the last thing a tick does** (2026-08-18, PR #489; the third of three since 2026-08-31 — see below):

```bash
bash ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/moderate/scripts/persist-log.sh --tick <tick>
```

**The persist runs three times, and each one buys something the others cannot** (2026-08-31, mission `stop-an-unattended-tick-from-waiting-on-a-person`). The account above described two; there are three, and the new one is the **first**:

1. **The opening**, immediately after `open-log` — so a tick that dies mid-run leaves its opening on the base. Without it the record that would show a stop is the record the stop prevents, which is why three consecutive ticks stuck at `requires_action` left no trace at all. `step-blocked-tick.sh` is what reads for it; neither half is worth anything alone.
2. **The closing**, `run.sh`'s last act — everything every step recorded, in one commit.
3. **The agent's**, after you have filed what `needs_agent` asked for — the `<step>-filed` lines, which the closing act could not have carried because you had not written them yet.

Each is idempotent and each unions by `(tick, step)`, so the later ones add without rewriting the earlier; each reports itself by name (`opening_persist` and `persist` in the run report, `persist-log-opening` and `persist-log` in the log) and a miss is `degraded` rather than fatal. **Not per step**: thirty-one commits an hour for a log is the noise the pull-request-per-tick design was refused for, and the stated price of the third is two commits an hour instead of one on an active repository. A tick killed **before** its first step still leaves nothing, which is outside this seam rather than papered over.

`run.sh` ran the persist as its closing act *before* you filed anything, because you act on `needs_agent` only after it returns — so without this second call every `<step>-filed` line dies with the container, and the dedups those lines exist to feed read an empty memory forever. It is idempotent: a tick that filed nothing gets `already_current` and writes no commit. Report its outcome in the session; it is deliberately not logged (recording it would need a third persist, and so on). If it comes back `degraded`, say so **by name** in the report — the lines are still in the checkout, and the next tick in the same container carries them up.

Finally report, in the session, one line per step: `<n> <step> <status> — <summary>`, then the counts. The report is the deliverable even when every step was a no-op.

## The tick log

`.workaholic/moderations/<YYYY-MM-DD>.md`, one `## <tick-id>` section per tick, one line per step. It is an **operational log, not an OKF knowledge artifact** — no `type:`, no `index.md`, the bundle root links the directory bare — and it is **append-only, never pruned by a machine**. Writer: `log-append.sh` (idempotent per `(tick, step)`). Reader: `log-read.sh`, which is how a step answers *"did an earlier tick already file this?"* instead of re-filing the same finding every hour:

```bash
bash ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/moderate/scripts/log-read.sh --step issue-triage --contains "#471"
```

Full rationale for both decisions (why it is not knowledge, why nothing prunes it): `plugins/workaholic/rules/workaholic.md`.

**The log lives on its own branch, not on `main`** (2026-09-01, issue #782, the developer's instruction). **A commit is a change to the development target**, and measured on a consuming repository's `main` — one calendar day, 275 commits — 138 touched only `.workaholic/` and five touched only the product; after squash-merging folded each unit's bookkeeping into its own commit, this log was the largest remaining author at three commits an hour. Its **content** is load-bearing and unchanged; its **home** moved. `gather/scripts/log-ref.sh` derives the branch once (`workaholic-log`, an orphan sharing no history with `main`, matching neither `work-*` nor `release/*` so the claim scan never sees it), `ensure-log-ref.sh` creates it on first use, and `.workaholic/moderations/` is **git-ignored** so an ordinary `git add -A` cannot put it back.

**The move has two halves and shipping one is worse than shipping neither.** A fresh container clones `main`, which no longer carries the log, so `step-open-log.sh` **hydrates** it (`hydrate-log.sh` — one fetch, the newest 30 day files, the branch's copy winning on the days it has) before any reader runs. Without that, `log-read.sh`'s dedup sets, `question-state.sh`, `record-answer.sh`, `condition-age.sh`, `filed-records.sh` and `step-blocked-tick.sh` would all answer *no earlier tick ever ran* and the whole dedup would re-fire hourly. A hydrate that failed is **named in that step's summary** and the tick continues — it over-reports rather than under-reports, the asymmetry the claim scan keeps.

**The findings take the other road.** `persist-log.sh --record <path>` still carries the tick's feedback records, and they go to **`main`**, in their own commit: a feedback record is knowledge — `/specificate` discovers it, `attributed-work.sh` walks it, a person opens it — and sending it to an operational branch to save one commit would hide the one thing here a reader of the product's history wants. The records' publication is **not load-bearing for the log**: either can fail without taking the other with it.

**The log is committed by the tick itself, as its closing act** — `run.sh` runs `persist-log.sh` after the last step, and that is the only thing in this skill that writes to a branch. A routine-fired tick runs in a fresh container cloned from the base, so a log left in the checkout would take every dedup's memory with it *and* leave an hourly unattended process with no audit trail; a hand-run never sees the failure, because its checkout survives. The seam is the **publish tree, directly** (`branching/scripts/open-publish-tree.sh` → `publish-tree-commit.sh` → `close-publish-tree.sh`): the caller's checkout is left byte-identical, no `work-*` branch is created, and no `publish-main` ref reaches origin, so the claim protocol never sees it. This is **not** the unattended-`main`-writer class `workaholic:ship` §7 refused — the append is not self-referential, it touches no branch a claim owns, and it merges nothing — and the reasoning is spelled out in `persist-log.sh`'s header alongside the rejected alternative (a pull request per tick: twenty-four a day asking a human to approve a line about what a machine already did). Concurrent ticks **union by `(tick, step)`** against a freshly fetched base rather than replaying a patch, so two containers on the same day both land — whole sections the base lacks, and, within a section it already carries, the individual entries it lacks. That line-wise half is what lets the agent's second persist above carry a `<step>-filed` line into a section that has already landed; a `(tick, step)` the base already has is never rewritten, so nothing a colleague's container recorded can be clobbered. A persist that did not reach the base is reported `degraded` **by name** and the log stays in the checkout for the next tick to carry up. The *last* persist's own line is written to the checkout and not to the base — the outcome is only known after the push, and the base already answers the question it would ask: the tick's section is there iff its persist succeeded.

## Where a cadence is declared, and what it says

A repository may declare **what it expects to keep being produced, and how often**. This is the one home for that declaration, and the reader (`cadence-state.sh`) and the step (`cadence-lapse`) reference it rather than restating it.

**The home is the repository's own configuration** — `.claude/settings.json`'s `env` block:

```json
{ "env": { "WORKAHOLIC_CADENCES": "moderation-log|.workaholic/moderations/*.md|2d; release-notes|.workaholic/release-notes/*.md|30d" } }
```

**The shape is three fields and nothing else**: a **name** (`[A-Za-z0-9._-]`, and the question's key), a **path pattern** the cadence's artifacts match (a git pathspec, repository-relative), and a **period** (`<n>d` days or `<n>h` hours). Entries are separated by `;` and fields by `|` — neither character occurs in a name, a glob or a period, which is what makes the split unambiguous without a parser. No credential, no command, no schedule expression: a schedule here would be a second copy of a routine's `cron_expression`, and a command would give an hourly unattended tick arbitrary code out of a file, which `step-workload-logs.sh` refused by name.

**Absent means nothing is declared**, and that is a real answer rather than a degradation — the `no_log_source` split. A repository that declares nothing is byte-identical to one before this existed: the step reports `skipped` with that reason, asks nobody, and supplies no event.

**No addressee, deliberately.** The declaration names no person, so the question the lapse draws is addressed to nobody rather than stamping an identity nothing verified (`step-base-health.sh`'s rule for an unmapped login). It is still visible on the root, which is where a person scanning the channel meets it.

**Why this home, and what the other two cost.** The discipline copied is `log_locator:`'s: optional, non-secret, absent means nothing declared, one reader, and a repository declaring nothing behaves as it did.

- **The area's own `README.md` frontmatter** — the closest existing precedent (`area-freshness.sh` already reads such frontmatter and already reports a `stale_days` nobody thresholds, which is precisely the number this supplies). It loses on a measurement: only **six of the twelve** `.workaholic/` areas carry a README at all, and `moderations/` — the area whose lapse measured this mission — is one of the six that do not. Hanging the field there would make the declaration mint the artifact it declares over, and it can never reach a cadence whose artifacts live outside `.workaholic/` (a generated site, a CI-written file), which is a large part of what is worth watching.
- **A new `.workaholic/` top-level area** — cleanest to read and validate, and the most expensive: an amendment in **both** lockstep sources, an OKF `type:`, an index entry and a write floor, for a value three fields long. And an area whose only writer is a human is exactly the shape the 2026-08-13 retirement removed three of. A declaration is configuration, not knowledge.
- **The repository's own configuration** wins on the 2026-08-29 precedent (`WORKAHOLIC_INBOUND_SLACK_CHANNEL`): a per-repository value with no artifact, no migration and no area amendment. Its stated cost is real — a list of triples in one variable is easy to mistype and has no validator — and it is paid where this repository always pays it: the reader **names** every malformed entry (`malformed_entry`, `bad_name`, `bad_period`) as `unreadable` with a null age rather than refusing, and `verify-cadence-lapse` pins the whole chain.

**The age is read from git, not from mtime, and that is a measurement.** A routine's container is a fresh clone and git stamps every checked-out file with the checkout's own time — measured 2026-08-31 inside a routine container on this repository: 1843 files carried the image's bake date and 481 the freshen's, and `.workaholic/moderations/2026-08-26.md` showed an mtime of that morning. An mtime reading answers `current` for every cadence, always, in exactly the environment this has to work in. So the age is the newest **commit** that touched the pattern, which survives a clone and is besides that the honest reading of *is it still being produced*: production here means committing. The caveat inverts and is stated rather than assumed — a file restored from history is a new commit and counts as production, and a producer that writes **outside** git cannot be measured here and must not be declared, because it would read `unreadable` forever.

## What the ask asked for, and what this is held to

The ask (issue #471) named nine steps (there are thirty-one: the release reads merged in, `stalled-units` and `closable-missions` were added 2026-08-23, `strategy-digest` — the integrated standup — on 2026-08-24, `direction-health`, `unanswered-asks` and `undrivable-units` on 2026-08-26, `undelivered-units`, `retire-claims`, `handoff-units` and `base-health` on 2026-08-27, `question-answers`, `thread-reconcile` and `standing-rulings` on 2026-08-28, `file-findings`, `drill-health`, `catchup-blocked` and `operator-pulls` on 2026-08-29, `raced-units` on 2026-08-30, and `cadence-lapse` and `blocked-tick` on 2026-08-31) and, at five points, met a decision the loop had already made. None of them is resolved by this skill quietly:

- **Step 8 inverts the propose bar.** `workaholic:specificate` states that missions, the queue and commits are *constraints, never triggers* — feedback is the only input that can originate a proposal, and the retired `[Propose Batch]` design was exactly a sweep of the repository's own state for something to propose. Proposing *from a strategy* is a reversal, not an addition, and it is ruled on in its own ticket.
- **`🟡 Proposing` collides with two standing shapes** — 🟡 is the handoff finish line, and the start post was retired on 2026-08-11 by the developer's order. Reintroducing a start post and reusing 🟡 are two separate rulings.
- **Step 4 races the claim protocol.** Pushing into an open pull request's branch was measured and refused for the deployment-plan refresh (`workaholic:ship` §7): the branch belongs to whoever holds its claim. This tick reports conflict state; it does not rebase somebody else's unit.
- **The asking step needs a surface and a clock.** No `AskUserQuestion` exists here, so asking means posting; "late-night" needs a timezone (the workspace's is Asia/Tokyo) and a boundary.
- **`scope: developer` multiplies steps 5, 6 and 7.** Those three read the *repository*, not the developer, so N copies would triage the same issues and post the same reminders every hour — the exact failure the `repository` scope was introduced for (2026-08-14, issue #451). The ask names `/setup-dev-routines`, so the split is a ruling, made in the routine-template ticket.

One measured fact about the premise: the repository holds **zero** strategies today (`strategy/scripts/list.sh` → `{"count": 0}`), so every step scoped "per strategy" reports `skipped` with that reason until the operator authors one. That is the honest outcome, not a defect.
